# Safety box: Servo feedback control

Read this before buying anything and again before every session. If any stop criterion is met, press the stop button, switch the motor battery pack off, unplug the USB cable, make the setup safe, and only then think about what happened.

This box carries over every limit of `projects/p04-electronics-bench/safety.md` and adds the limits for a moving actuator. Where the two differ, this box is the stricter one for this project: p04 forbids motors altogether; here exactly one small motor or micro servo is allowed, inside the limits below.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hazards** | A feedback loop with the wrong sign (positive feedback) or too much gain drives the output hard into its end stop or into a sustained oscillation; the motor then stalls, draws its full stall current and heats the motor, the driver, the wires and the cells. A moving shaft or servo horn pinches fingertips, catches hair, loose sleeves and wires, and flings anything attached to it; a propeller, fan or blade on the shaft would cut. A short circuit across a battery pack or a USB port makes wires and cells hot enough to burn skin and start a fire. A motor sharing the board's supply can brown out the board, which then stops controlling while the driver output stays on. A charged capacitor can discharge through you or burst if reverse-connected or over-voltaged. Cut wire ends are sharp. Small parts are a choking hazard for children and pets. |
| **Energy limits** | **Battery or USB power only, never mains.** **Motor (or servo) supply ≤ 6 V**, from its own AA pack (4 × NiMH, or 3 × alkaline; never 4 fresh alkaline cells, which can reach 6.4 V), measured with the multimeter before every session. **Board and logic from USB (≤ 5 V, ≤ 500 mA)**, as in p04; the motor never runs from the board's 3.3 V regulator or from a GPIO pin. **One motor or micro servo, stall current ≤ 500 mA at the supply used**, with a **0.5 A resettable fuse** in the + lead of the motor pack. The 500 mA stall limit keeps the motor at about 3 W or less at 6 V; the fuse is only a backstop: it holds 0.5 A indefinitely and trips near 1 A (about 6 W at 6 V), so it catches a short or an over-size motor, not a 3 W overload. **The stall current is measured, if not stated, only by commanding the motor or servo against a fixed stop for ≤ 1 s, never by holding the shaft or horn by hand; a reading above 500 mA rejects the motor.** **No analog pin above the board's supply**: measure a servo's feedback wire before connecting it, and use a divider if it is higher. **Output speed ≤ about 300 rpm.** **Nothing on the shaft or horn except a light pointer arm** (card or a craft stick, ≤ 5 g, ≤ 80 mm): **no propellers, no fans, no blades, no wheels, no weights.** **A software duty limit (Option B, |duty| ≤ 0.6) or command limit (Option A, ±60° from centre), a travel limit, and a stop button**, all in the firmware and **all tested before the first closed-loop run**. Every p04 limit still holds: no capacitors above 25 V or 1000 µF (discharge through 1 kΩ, observe polarity), no lithium cells or charging circuits, no soldering required, no analog pin above the board's supply voltage. |
| **Controls** | Build and change every circuit with the power disconnected (motor pack switched off **and** USB unplugged). Common ground between the board, the driver (or servo) and the motor pack; check every node with continuity before powering, and check there is no direct path from the pack's + to ground. Mount the servo or motor firmly (tape, block or clamp) so it cannot walk or fall. Before any closed loop, in this order and logged: (1) **stop test** - with an open-loop duty of 0.3 (or a 10° command) running, press the stop button; the output must go to zero within one sample and stay there; (2) **limit test** - command a duty of 1.0 (or 90°) in software; the logged output must be clipped at the limit; (3) **sign test** - a small positive duty (or command) must move the measured angle in the positive direction; if not, power off and swap the motor leads (or the sign in the firmware); a wrong sign is positive feedback; (4) **travel-limit test** - turn the pot by hand (motor pack off) past the travel limit; the firmware must report a stop. Start every closed-loop session with the gain at half the designed value and small steps (≤ 15°). **Fingers, hair and loose sleeves clear of the shaft while the loop is running**; touch the mechanism only with the motor pack switched off. Keep one hand on the stop button and the pack switch within reach. In the first minute of each run, and after each run, touch-test the motor, the driver and the fuse with the pack off: warm means stop. Never leave a powered loop unattended. |
| **Supervision** | None needed for adults. Children and teenagers work with an adult present who has read this box; small parts kept away from young children and pets. |
| **Location** | Indoors on a dry, non-conductive, clutter-free table, away from sinks and drinks; good light; nothing within reach of the pointer's swing; the computer within reach of the USB cable so it does not pull the board off the bench; the pack switch and the stop button at the front edge of the table. |
| **Regulations** | None apply at these voltages. Dispose of batteries according to local battery-recycling rules. |
| **Stop criteria** | The pointer oscillates and does not settle, or the output hits an end stop or the travel limit; the motor buzzes or hums without moving for more than a second (stall); any part (motor, driver, fuse, wires, cells) warm to the touch; the fuse trips; any smell of burning or hot plastic; smoke; the pack measures above 6.0 V; the board resets, disconnects from USB or stops logging while the motor moves; the stop button or the limit test does not behave as in the controls; anything has been attached to the shaft other than the pointer arm; a swollen or leaking cell or capacitor; you are tired or rushed. |
| **Disposal** | NiMH and alkaline cells to a battery-recycling point; damaged motors, servos, driver modules and boards to electronic-waste collection; wire offcuts and card in the bin. Never throw cells into household waste or a fire. |
| **Not permitted in this project** | Mains wiring of any kind, wall adapters, bench supplies or opened chargers; any motor supply above 6 V; any motor or servo with stall current above 500 mA, or more than one at a time; brushless motors, ESCs, drone or RC-car power systems; **propellers, fans, blades, wheels, arms longer than 80 mm or heavier than 5 g, or any load on the shaft**; running the motor from the board's regulator or a GPIO pin; removing the fuse, the software limits or the stop button, or raising the duty limit above 0.6 (or the command limit above ±60°) to "make it faster"; closing the loop before the four tests pass; touching the mechanism while the loop runs; lithium cells, lithium charging or protection circuits; capacitors above 25 V or 1000 µF; leaving a powered loop unattended. |

Safety is a hard requirement in the rubric: a report without a completed safety box and the logged results of the four pre-run tests is not graded.
