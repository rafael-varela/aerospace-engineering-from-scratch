# Safety box: Electronics bench lab

Read this before buying anything and again before every session. If any stop criterion is met, stop, disconnect the power, make the setup safe, and only then think about what happened.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hazards** | A short circuit across a battery or a USB port makes wires, cells and parts hot enough to burn skin and start a fire. An LED connected without a resistor, or a resistor carrying too much current, overheats. A multimeter left on its current range and touched across a supply blows its fuse (or, unfused, the meter). A charged capacitor can discharge through you or burst if reverse-connected or over-voltaged. Water near powered electronics shorts them. Cut wire ends and component leads are sharp and can fly when clipped. Small parts are a choking hazard for children and pets. |
| **Energy limits** | **Battery or USB power only, never mains.** **Supply ≤ 9 V DC** (USB 5 V, a board's 3.3 V rail, alkaline or NiMH AA packs up to 6 V, or a 9 V battery). **Current ≤ 500 mA**, which is what a USB port supplies; the circuits in this lab draw under 20 mA. **Every LED through a series resistor** (the lesson's design gives ≤ 10 mA). **No lithium-cell charging circuits**, and no loose lithium cells. **No capacitors rated above 25 V or larger than 1000 µF**; discharge every capacitor through a 1 kΩ resistor before touching or removing it, and observe its polarity. **No soldering required.** No analog pin ever sees more than the board's own supply voltage. |
| **Controls** | Build and change every circuit with the power disconnected (USB unplugged, battery holder switched off or a cell removed). Before powering, check every node with the meter's continuity mode and look for a direct path from the supply rail to ground. In the first minute after powering, touch-test each resistor, LED and the board: anything warm means power off. Keep the multimeter leads in the V/Ω jack; move the red lead to the mA jack only for the current measurement, and move it back immediately afterwards. Thermistor sealed in a zip bag; the mugs of water on a tray at the far side of the bench from the board and the computer; dry hands when touching the circuit. Warm tap water only (never boiling or kettle-hot). Clip leads pointing down into a hand or a bin. If you choose to solder (not required): a stand for the iron, safety glasses, ventilation or a fan pulling fumes away, lead-free solder, wash hands afterwards, iron unplugged when you leave the bench. |
| **Supervision** | None needed for adults. Children and teenagers work with an adult present; small parts kept away from young children and pets. |
| **Location** | Indoors on a dry, non-conductive, clutter-free table, away from sinks and drinks; good light; the computer or power bank within reach of the USB cable so it does not pull the board off the bench. |
| **Regulations** | None apply at these voltages. Dispose of batteries according to local battery-recycling rules. |
| **Stop criteria** | Any part hot to the touch; any smell of burning or hot plastic; smoke; a battery or USB power bank that becomes warm; the computer reporting a USB over-current or the port disconnecting; a reading above 9 V or above 500 mA, or a current reading much larger than predicted; water on the bench or on the circuit; a swollen or leaking cell or capacitor; you are tired or rushed. |
| **Disposal** | Alkaline and NiMH cells to a battery-recycling point; damaged LEDs, resistors and boards to electronic-waste collection; wire offcuts in the bin. Never throw cells into household waste or a fire. |
| **Not permitted in this project** | Mains wiring of any kind; opening wall adapters, chargers or power supplies; anything above 9 V; lithium cells outside their own device, lithium charging or protection circuits; high-voltage modules (boost converters, flyback, Geiger-counter or plasma kits); capacitors above 25 V or 1000 µF; motors, relays or loads drawing more than 100 mA; LEDs without a resistor; lasers of any class other than an LED indicator; measuring current across a supply (meter as a short circuit); powering the board from more than one source at a time. |

Safety is a hard requirement in the rubric: a report without a completed safety box is not graded.
