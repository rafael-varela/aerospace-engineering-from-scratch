# Safety box: Draining bottle and water manometer

Read this before buying anything and again before every session. If any stop criterion is met, stop: switch the fan or hair dryer off **at the wall socket** and unplug it (only with dry hands, standing on a dry floor), move it away from the water, and only then mop up and think about what happened.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hazards** | Water and mains electricity together: a hair dryer or fan falling into a basin, or a cable, plug or socket in spilled water (electric shock). Slipping on a wet floor. Fingers or hair caught in a fan. Hot air and burns if a hair dryer is used on a heated setting; overheating if its intake is blocked. Cuts from a knife or drill bit while making the hole. A bottle that is squeezed, capped or pressurised and squirts or bursts. Swallowing or inhaling water by filling the manometer by mouth. Food colouring staining. |
| **Energy limits** | **Water at room temperature only, no more than 2 L drawn in the whole session** (the lesson uses about 1.5 L in the bottle and 20 mL in the manometer; the three draining runs reuse the same water, caught in a clean basin and poured back with the jug, never fresh water for each run). **Fans guarded with a finger-proof grille and drawing at most 30 W** (read the rating plate). **Hair dryer on its cool (no-heat) setting only.** The bottle is driven by gravity alone: about 0.2 m of water head, 2 kPa. The airflows are a few to fifteen metres per second; the pressures read are below 150 Pa. |
| **Controls** | **The water and the mains appliance are never on the same table**: the bottle drains in a sink, bathtub or basin on the floor; the manometer's water sits in the closed U of the tube, taped to its board, with the board on a towel; the hair dryer or fan is on a separate dry table or held by hand at the board's edge, never above the basin. **Mop any spill before switching anything on**, and dry your hands. Cables run on the dry side, away from the basin, with the plug in a socket above floor level; use a socket protected by a residual-current device (RCD/GFCI) where you have one. The hair dryer's heat switch is set to cool before it is plugged in, its intake kept clear. The fan's grille is checked intact before use; nothing is pushed through it. The hole is made before filling, on a cutting board, cutting away from the hand that holds the bottle, with a drill bit turned by hand; never with a heated nail, flame or hob (melting PET gives off fumes). Fill the manometer with a syringe or by dipping one end into a cup, never by mouth. |
| **Supervision** | None needed for an adult. A child does this only with an adult who handles the mains appliance and makes the hole. |
| **Location** | Draining: a kitchen sink, bathtub or a basin on a hard floor, with towels ready. Manometer: a table with a towel under the board, at least an arm's length from any sink or basin; the air source on a separate dry surface. Not on carpet next to sockets, not in a bathroom while anything is plugged in near the bath. |
| **Regulations** | None apply. Use appliances only as their manufacturers intend; do not open or modify a fan or a hair dryer. |
| **Stop criteria** | **Water on or near a cable, plug, socket or appliance.** Any spill on the floor (stop and mop). A smell of hot plastic, a hair dryer that blows warm air on its cool setting, or a fan that is hot to the touch. A cracked or missing fan grille. The bottle leaking from anywhere but the hole, or bulging. Water reaching the end of the manometer tube or entering the straw (the reading is lost and the water can be blown out: move the source back). Anyone touching the appliance with wet hands. |
| **Disposal** | Water down the sink. The bottle with its hole goes to plastic recycling once dry. Tubing and straws can be kept for later projects. |
| **Not permitted in this project** | **Compressed gas** of any kind (aerosol cans, CO₂ cartridges, compressors, bicycle pumps, compressed-air dusters); **pressurised containers** (no squeezing, capping or pumping a bottle to drive the flow; no blowing into the manometer or the bottle); **hot water** or a hair dryer on a heated setting; melting the hole with a heated nail, soldering iron or flame; more than 2 L of water; a fan without a finger-proof grille or rated above 30 W; any **mains appliance near spilled water** or on the same table as the bottle or the basin; glass containers; filling anything by mouth. |

Safety is a hard requirement in the rubric: a report without a completed safety box is not graded.
