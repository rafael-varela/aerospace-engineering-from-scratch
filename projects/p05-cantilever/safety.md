# Safety box: Deflection of a clamped metal strip

Read this before buying anything and again before every session. If any stop criterion is met, stop, lift the load off by hand (supporting it from below), loosen nothing until the strip is unloaded, and only then think about what happened.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hazards** | The hanging mass falling onto feet, a pet or the floor when the string, hook or clamp fails. The clamp slipping and the strip springing up towards the face. A strip that snaps (a plastic ruler, a hardened steel rule, any strip loaded past its safe load) throwing fragments or a sharp end. Fingers pinched in the clamp or between the load and the table. Sharp, burred cut ends of metal strip. A table that tips when a load hangs off its edge. |
| **Energy limits** | **Total hanging mass ≤ 2 kg**, in every case and with every specimen. Below that, never more than the safe load computed for your strip, from the clamp stress 6Pa/(bh²) ≤ F_ty/2 (956 g for the lesson's 25 × 3 mm aluminium strip at a = 290 mm; the lesson stops at 800 g). At 800 g the strip stores about 0.06 J of elastic energy; a 2 kg mass falling 0.75 m from a table edge to the floor carries about 15 J, which is why nothing hangs over feet. |
| **Controls** | **Nothing is hung over feet**: stand to the side, and keep a box, folded towel or cushion under the load a few centimetres below it. **The clamp is checked before each load**: tighten it, confirm the strip has not moved against a pencil mark at the clamp edge. Add and remove loads gently by hand, supporting the bag from below; never drop a load onto the hanger. The hanger sits in a filed notch so it cannot slide off the end. Cut ends deburred with a file or covered with tape. **Safety glasses whenever loading a strip that can snap**: any plastic ruler, a steel rule, or any strip loaded near its safe load; recommended for every session. Keep the face out of the plane above the strip while loading. The table is sturdy and does not rock with the top load hanging from it. |
| **Supervision** | None needed for an adult. A child does this only with an adult doing the loading and checking the clamp. |
| **Location** | Indoors, on a sturdy table or workbench with a clear floor below the load and no one sitting at that side of the table. Not on a glass-topped table, not on a folding table, not over a bed, a keyboard or anything fragile. |
| **Regulations** | None apply. |
| **Stop criteria** | **Visible permanent set**: the unloaded reading after removing a load differs from the one before it by more than 0.5 mm (or by more than three times the dial gauge's reading uncertainty). **Cracking**, whitening, a creak or a click from the strip or the clamp. The strip moves in the clamp (pencil mark displaced). The table rocks or shifts. The hanger loop slides or the string frays. Deflection that keeps growing at constant load (creep). Anyone or any pet under or next to the load. |
| **Disposal** | Coins, water and bags back to household use. A strip with permanent set can be kept for other projects or recycled as aluminium or steel scrap; broken plastic in household waste, wrapped so sharp edges cannot cut. |
| **Not permitted in this project** | **Loading glass, carbon-fibre rods, wooden rulers or anything else that can splinter**; brittle clear plastic rulers (polystyrene, acrylic); any hanging mass over 2 kg or over the strip's computed safe load; hanging anything over feet; standing on, leaning on or sitting on the loaded strip or the table edge; loading by pulling down by hand or by bouncing; clamping to a shelf, a windowsill or a chair. |

Safety is a hard requirement in the rubric: a report without a completed safety box is not graded.
