## What Phase 07 must achieve

Engineering thermodynamics and introductory heat transfer at the level of a second-year thermodynamics course and a first heat-transfer course (MIT 16.unified thermodynamics, 2.005/2.006, 10.40 for equilibrium; Lienhard scope for heat transfer), taught through aerospace systems: pressurant tanks, propellant properties, compressors and gas-turbine cycles, cabin air, combustors, rocket-chamber equilibrium, nozzle and heat-shield walls, avionics cooling and spacecraft radiators. The organising idea of the phase is *conservation plus the second law*: every lesson writes its mass and energy balance first, states what generates entropy, and checks its main result by an independent route (a closed form, a limit, a second method, a conservation law closing to round-off, or a measurement). A learner who finishes can compute ideal-gas properties from NASA polynomials, apply the first law to closed systems and control volumes including unsteady ones, read and interpolate real-substance tables, compute entropy generation and exergy destruction, analyse ideal and real Brayton cycles, do psychrometrics for a cabin, compute adiabatic flame temperatures and chemical equilibrium by Gibbs minimization, solve conduction problems numerically, use convection correlations, compute view factors and radiative exchange, and measure a cooling curve at home and compare it with lumped and distributed models, with a tested Python module for each.

## Property data (shared; use exactly these)

All property data come from free, verified sources. Copy the data you need into your lesson's `starter/main.py` and `solution.py` as given constants (identical in both files), with a comment naming the source. Lessons are self-contained: never import from another lesson.

1. **NASA Glenn 9-coefficient polynomials.** The orchestrator has extracted the coefficients, unrounded, from `data/thermo.inp` of NASA CEA (`https://github.com/nasa/cea`, Apache-2.0), the database documented in McBride, Zehe & Gordon, NASA/TP-2002-211556. The extracted file is given to you at a scratchpad path in your instructions (`nasa9_p07.py`) with species N2, O2, Ar, CO2, H2O, CO, H2, OH, H, O, NO, N, NO2, He, CH4, C3H8, Jet-A(g) (C12H23) and H2O(L). Use only the intervals you need. Forms:
   - cp/R = a1 T⁻² + a2 T⁻¹ + a3 + a4 T + a5 T² + a6 T³ + a7 T⁴
   - h/(RT) = −a1 T⁻² + a2 ln(T)/T + a3 + a4 T/2 + a5 T²/3 + a6 T³/4 + a7 T⁴/5 + b1/T
   - s°/R = −a1 T⁻²/2 − a2 T⁻¹ + a3 ln T + a4 T + a5 T²/2 + a6 T³/3 + a7 T⁴/4 + b2, at p° = 1 bar
   - h includes the enthalpy of formation: h(298.15 K) reproduces `Hf298` to about 2 J/mol (the database used R = 8.314510; we use the CODATA 2018 value R = 8.31446261815324 J/(mol·K); state this tolerance in tests).
   - Dry air model (fixed for the whole phase): mole fractions N2 0.7808, O2 0.2095, Ar 0.0093, CO2 0.0004 (M = 28.965 g/mol).
2. **Water, ammonia and CO2 tables.** Saturated (temperature table), superheated and compressed-liquid tables from Yan, *Introduction to Engineering Thermodynamics* (LibreTexts), Appendices A, B and D, whose values were generated from the NIST Chemistry WebBook (SRD 69, Lemmon, McLinden & Friend). The orchestrator has saved them as CSV files at a scratchpad path given in your instructions (`p07data/`). Embed the subset you need. NIST's own site cannot be loaded from the authoring environment, so cite the Yan appendix by `@id` and name NIST SRD 69 as the original source in prose.
3. **Heat-transfer properties.** Air (Table A.6, gases at 1 atm), metals (Table A.1), non-metals (Table A.2) and liquids (Table A.3) from Lienhard & Lienhard, *A Heat Transfer Textbook*, 6th ed. (v6.00, 2024), Appendix A. The orchestrator has saved a text extraction of the book at a scratchpad path given in your instructions (`dl/ahtt.txt`); read the tables and the correlation chapters there. The book is free to download and read but is copyrighted (personal and non-profit instructional use): use its property values as data with a citation, write your own explanations, never copy its text or figures.
4. **Constants.** R = 8.31446261815324 J/(mol·K), σ = 5.670374419 × 10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴), k_B = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K, g₀ = 9.80665 m/s², p° = 1 bar for NASA data and 101 325 Pa for 1 atm. Say which one you use.

Never invent a property value. If a value you need is not in these sources or another verified free source in `references/phase-07.md`, derive it, or label it "typical, illustrative" and do not test against it.

## Physical experiment (lesson 15)

- Lesson 15 owns `projects/p07-cooling-curve/` with `bom.yaml` (three tiers: `minimum_cost`, `recommended`, `enhanced`, as in the earlier kits; prices as ranges with the date checked; `reuse_from` naming the p00 and p04 kits: `p00-calibration`, `p00-first-experiment`, `p04-electronics-bench`), `safety.md` (the full safety box in the format of `projects/p05-cantilever/safety.md`), `procedure.md`, `report-template.md` and `data/README.md`, plus `data/synthetic/` holding the seeded synthetic dataset.
- Apparatus: a small metal object (a large steel washer, an aluminium block or a short aluminium bar) warmed in tap-hot water **no hotter than 60 °C**, lifted out **with tongs**, dried, and hung or set on an insulating stand to cool in still indoor air. Temperature logged with a kitchen probe thermometer read at fixed intervals, or with the 10 kΩ NTC thermistor on the `p04-electronics-bench` microcontroller (USB or battery powered), taped or clamped into a drilled hole or against the surface. The prediction (time constant τ = ρcV/(hA) from h estimated with the lesson 13 natural-convection correlation plus linearized radiation from lesson 14, with uncertainties) is **written down and dated before measuring**. The learner fits a lumped-capacitance exponential (linear fit of ln θ and a nonlinear fit), checks the Biot number, and compares against a 1-D distributed model (slab or cylinder series solution, or finite differences from lesson 12).
- Hard limits stated in `safety.md` and repeated in the lesson: **water at no more than 60 °C** (checked with the thermometer before the object goes in); **no boiling water, kettle at the boil, stove, oven, flame, heat gun, hot plate, microwave heating of metal, or soldering iron**; **the object is handled with tongs** while hot; **battery or USB power only** for any electronics (no mains-powered heaters or circuits); no glass containers; keep water away from the microcontroller and the computer; stop if the object is too hot to touch through a cloth, if the water exceeds 60 °C, or if any electronics get wet.
- The learner without a kit completes the lesson with a supplied seeded synthetic dataset (generator in `solution.py`, also given in the starter), labelled **SYNTHETIC** in every file name, header line, table caption and plot title where it appears.
