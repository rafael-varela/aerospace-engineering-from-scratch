# Phase 06: Dynamics & Vibrations

Second-year engineering dynamics and third-year vibrations, at the level of MIT 16.07 and 2.003 or a Meriam, Hibbeler, Thomson or Inman course, taught through aircraft and spacecraft and organised around one idea: *kinematics, kinetics, and an independent check*. Every lesson states the frame it works in, inertial or rotating, and which axes. It writes the kinematics, how positions, velocities and accelerations are related in that frame, before the kinetics, the forces and moments that cause them. Then it checks its main result by an independent route: a closed form against `scipy.integrate.solve_ivp`, conservation of energy or angular momentum to a stated tolerance, Lagrange against Newton–Euler for the same system, a DCM against a quaternion, a rotating-frame against an inertial-frame integration, modal superposition against direct integration, a finite-element eigenproblem against the closed-form frequency equation, an analytic Grms against a time-domain simulation, and in lesson 19 a model against your own measurement. By the end you can describe particle motion in Cartesian, polar, cylindrical and path coordinates, and compute manoeuvre load factors. You can use work–energy and impulse–momentum for impacts and arrested landings, and work in rotating frames, including the Earth's. You can analyse planar linkages and rolling and sliding bodies. You can represent and propagate 3-D attitude with Euler angles, DCMs and quaternions, integrate Euler's equations and explain spin stability. You can derive equations of motion by Lagrange and Hamilton. You can compute the free, forced, modal, continuous and random response of vibrating structures, design a vibration isolator, build a small multibody model, and identify the frequency and damping of a real pendulum with uncertainties. You get a tested Python module for each of these. The mathematics is not new: Phases 01–03 supplied it, and Phase 04 supplied the physics. What is new is engineering dynamics: moving and rotating frames, rigid bodies in two and three dimensions, attitude representations, analytical mechanics, and vibration of discrete and continuous systems under deterministic and random loads.

One lesson has a physical experiment (marked 🔬): the pendulum system-identification home lab. A learner without the kit completes it with the seeded synthetic dataset supplied, which is labelled **SYNTHETIC** wherever it appears; a calculation or a simulation is never presented as a measurement. Every other lesson's part 4 is a computational experiment, such as a parameter sweep, a sensitivity study, a comparison of two methods, or an analysis of a public or synthetic dataset. Lesson 12 is marked ★: it is optional, and nothing later in the phase depends on it.

## The project kit

The kit lives in `projects/p06-pendulum-sysid/` with a `bom.yaml` (three cost tiers, `minimum_cost`, `recommended` and `enhanced`, with dated price ranges, reusing the `p04-pendulum` string pendulum and, where it is needed, the kitchen scale from `p00-calibration`), `safety.md` (the full safety box), `procedure.md`, `report-template.md` and `data/README.md` (the raw-data format, and the committed `SYNTHETIC_seed<seed>_swing.csv` for learners without the kit). Read `safety.md` before buying anything and again before every session. The kit's `safety.md` is authoritative. The limits below summarise the hard rules that every version of this kit must keep, and nothing in the kit may relax them. The prediction, with its uncertainty, is written down and dated before the first swing, raw data are never edited, and the report states which numbers were measured, which were computed, and which are synthetic.

- **`p06-pendulum-sysid`** ([folder](../../projects/p06-pendulum-sysid/), lesson 19). The `p04-pendulum` string pendulum, reused. At the minimum-cost tier you film it with a phone against a protractor or grid and read the angle frame by frame, or with any free video-tracking tool you prefer. At the recommended tier you log the phone's own gyroscope with a free sensor-logging app, with the phone *as the bob* in a padded pouch, or film a high-contrast marker. At the enhanced tier a small IMU breakout on the `p04-electronics-bench` microcontroller logs over USB. You predict the frequency from the length and g, with the large-amplitude correction, and an order-of-magnitude damping ratio, all with uncertainties, before the first swing. Then you estimate the damped frequency by zero crossings and an FFT peak, the damping ratio by logarithmic decrement, and both together by nonlinear least squares with uncertainties checked by a seeded bootstrap or repeat runs, and decide whether the decay is viscous, Coulomb or quadratic. Hard limits:
  - string length ≤ 2 m;
  - bob mass ≤ 0.2 kg (a phone used as the bob only in a padded pouch, with the total within 0.2 kg);
  - release amplitude ≤ 45°;
  - the swing arc clear of people, pets, screens and glass;
  - no powered drive of the pendulum.

Nothing in this phase involves power tools, pressure vessels, combustion, motor firing, high-speed unguarded rotors, projectiles, or flight. The artillery shell, the arrested landing, the spinning upper stage and the landing-gear drop test are computational experiments only. **A report without a completed safety box is not graded.**

## Running the code

From the repo root, `pytest curriculum/phase-06/lesson-NN-<slug>` tests your `starter/main.py`. `AEFS_IMPL=solution pytest curriculum/phase-06/lesson-NN-<slug>` runs the same tests against the reference solution in `solutions/phase-06/lesson-NN-<slug>/solution.py`. Tests check properties rather than single numbers:
- conservation residuals (energy, angular-momentum magnitude, the inertial angular-momentum vector) below a stated tolerance;
- orthonormality and normalisation (CᵀC = I, det C = +1, |q| = 1);
- agreement between two methods (closed form and `solve_ivp`, Lagrange and Newton–Euler, DCM and quaternion, rotating and inertial frames, modal and direct integration, finite elements and the frequency equation, analytic and time-domain Grms);
- limiting cases (zero damping, zero spin, infinite stiffness, small angle, an axisymmetric body);
- symmetry and dimensional sanity, and convergence order where a numerical method is used.

The code uses NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib and SymPy only. No dynamics, multibody, rotation or vibration library is used in tested code; `scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation` appears only as an independent check in the tests and in the "Compare" steps of lessons 07 and 08. Anything random is seeded. Tests need no network and write files only to a temporary directory. `python scripts/check_lessons.py` checks every lesson's structure.

## The exam and the mini-project

**Phase exam and mini-project:** [assessments/phase-06/](../../assessments/phase-06/). The exam has four parts. Part A covers concepts: frames and kinematics, rigid bodies in three dimensions, analytical mechanics, and vibration. Part B covers analysis by hand over every lesson from 01 to 18; its question on Hamiltonian mechanics is marked ★ and optional, like lesson 12. Part C is computation: a tested torque-free rigid-body propagator, checked by conservation laws, by the inertial angular-momentum vector and by an axisymmetric closed form. Part D is engineering interpretation. Its first question asks you to interpret your own `p06-pendulum-sysid` measurement; a seeded synthetic dataset is supplied for learners without a kit. Its second question reviews a synthetic vibration analysis sheet for unit, formula and isolator errors, and tests the pendulum kit's safety limits. The exam has not been piloted, so no time limit is suggested. Choose one in advance and record it. Results are reported per competency: (1) reasoning, (2) analysis, (3) computation, (4) interpretation. The worked solutions and pass thresholds are in [solutions/phase-06/exam/](../../solutions/phase-06/exam/worked-solutions.md). The mini-project is the pendulum system-identification report from lesson 19, graded with [the rubric](../../assessments/phase-06/grading-rubric.md). Seeded unseen cases for the computational lessons are planned in [parameterized-cases/](../../assessments/phase-06/parameterized-cases/README.md). Outcomes are recorded as *practice completed*, *self-assessed mastery* or *externally reviewed mastery*. None of these is a credential, and nothing in this phase qualifies anyone to analyse, test or approve real aircraft or spacecraft dynamics, vibration or loads.
