## 1. The engineering problem

Every number in an aerodynamics report, from a lift curve to a drag polar, began as something else: a voltage from a strain gauge, a reading on a scale, a pressure on a manometer. A wind-tunnel balance does not know which way the wind blows. It measures forces along its own axes, usually fixed to the model, and a moment about its own centre, usually somewhere inside the sting. Turning those readings into lift, drag and pitching moment is a rotation, a moment transfer and a division by the dynamic pressure. Each step is simple. Each one, done carelessly, produces numbers that look reasonable and are wrong: a forgotten rotation turns a small positive drag into a large negative one, a forgotten offset moves the aerodynamic centre by a centimetre, and a tenth of a degree of angle error changes the drag by a sixth.

This lesson builds the calculator that does those steps for every later lesson in the phase, and the uncertainty budget that says how far to trust its output. The case is a section model of the reference trainer's NACA 2412 airfoil (chord 0.30 m, span 0.60 m) in a 2-D tunnel at 35 m/s. The readings are **SYNTHETIC**: the solution's `synthetic_balance_data(seed=42)` invents a plausible section, computes what a balance would read, and adds noise of stated size. That lets you check the reduction against a known truth, which you never have with real data. The engineering use is the trainer: at cruise (55 m/s, 750 kg, S = 12.0 m²) it needs C_L = 0.331, and the section data say at what angle the airfoil gives that.

Success criteria, written first: noise-free readings reduce to the generator's truth to round-off; the aerodynamic centre is the same whichever reference point you reduce to; the noisy reduction agrees with the truth within its stated uncertainty; first-order propagation agrees with Monte Carlo; and the budget names which instrument limits each coefficient.

## 2. Understand and derive

### 2.1 Recap: why coefficients (`P08.05`)

The aerodynamic force on a body of fixed shape at fixed attitude depends on ρ, V, a length l, the viscosity μ and the speed of sound a. Five dimensional quantities built from three units (kg, m, s) leave, by the Buckingham Π theorem of `P08.05`, three independent groups once the force itself is included:

F/(½ρV²l²) = f(Re, M),  Re = ρVl/μ = Vl/ν,  M = V/a.

So a force coefficient is a function of the Reynolds number and the Mach number only. A model at the same Re and M as the aeroplane has the same coefficients. Using **q = ½ρV²** (the dynamic pressure, in pascals) and a stated reference area S and length c:

**c_l = L/(qS),  c_d = D/(qS),  c_m = M/(qSc).**   (1)

Lower case is for a 2-D section (S = c × span, forces per unit span ÷ qc); upper case for a 3-D wing or aircraft. The reference trainer at cruise has q = 1852.8 Pa, Re_c = 4.52 × 10⁶ and M = 0.162. The synthetic tunnel model has q = 737.4 Pa, Re_c = 6.98 × 10⁵ and M = 0.102. To match the trainer's Re with a 0.30 m chord in room air would need 226 m/s, M = 0.66: Reynolds and Mach numbers cannot both be matched in an ordinary tunnel, which is the first fact of experimental aerodynamics.

### 2.2 Body axes to wind axes

Put x along the chord (aft positive) and z normal to it (up). The balance reports a **normal force N** along +z and an **axial force A** along +x. The stream comes at angle α (nose-up positive) to the chord. Lift is perpendicular to the stream and drag along it:

```
              z  ↑ N                    L ↑   ↗ stream direction is rotated by α from x
                 |                        |  /
   ──────────────●────────→ x (chord)     | / α
                 A →                      |/________ D
```

Projecting F = (A, N) on the stream direction (cos α, sin α) and on its normal (−sin α, cos α):

**L = N cos α − A sin α,  D = N sin α + A cos α.**   (2)

It is a rotation, so L² + D² = N² + A², and the inverse is the rotation by −α. Two consequences matter in the lab. At α = 4° on the synthetic model, A is *negative* (−0.0377 qS): the lift vector, tilted forward relative to the chord normal, pulls the model forward more than the drag pushes it back. That forward pull is leading-edge suction. And because N is a hundred times D, any error in α leaks N into D: differentiating (2) at fixed N and A,

**∂D/∂α = N cos α − A sin α = L,  so  u(c_d) ≈ c_l · u_α  (u_α in radians).**   (3)

At c_l = 0.64 a 0.1° error is 0.0011 in c_d, 16 % of the drag. Drag is the hardest coefficient to measure on a balance for exactly this reason; wake surveys (the method behind Ladson's NACA 0012 data in `P10.06`) avoid it.

### 2.3 Moment transfer

A force F = (F_x, F_z) acting at r has the moment (r − r_P) × F about P. With x aft and z up, its nose-up component is (z − z_P)F_x − (x − x_P)F_z. A balance gives N and A acting through its centre O plus a couple M_O, so the moment about any point P is

**M_P = M_O + (x_P − x_O) N − (z_P − z_O) A.**   (4)

The sign check: an upward N acting behind P (x_O > x_P) gives −(x_O − x_P)N < 0, nose-down, as a sketch shows. A pure couple (N = A = 0) is the same about every point.

The synthetic balance centre is at x = 0.120 m (0.40c) and z = −0.020 m, 20 mm below the chord line, as on a sting. The quarter chord is 45 mm ahead and 20 mm above it. The vertical offset multiplies A: forgetting it changes c_m by −z_O c_a/c, an error of −0.0025 at 4° (z_O < 0 and c_a < 0), and moves the apparent aerodynamic centre from 0.2481c to 0.2573c.

### 2.4 The aerodynamic centre

For a thin airfoil there is a point, the **aerodynamic centre** (a.c.), about which the pitching moment does not change with incidence (`P10.04` proves it is the quarter chord in thin-airfoil theory). By (4) with both points on the chord, M_ref = M_ac + (x_ref − x_ac)N, or in coefficients

**c_m,ref = c_m,ac + (x_ref − x_ac)/c · c_n,  so  x_ac = x_ref − c · dc_m,ref/dc_n.**   (5)

The slope of c_m against c_n locates the a.c., and changing the reference point changes that slope by exactly Δx/c. The textbook form uses c_l instead of c_n; they differ by c_l(1 − cos α) + c_d sin α, second order at small α. The code uses c_n.

### 2.5 Uncertainty

Each coefficient is a function y = f(x) of five readings x = (N, A, M, α, q) with standard uncertainties u_j. To first order (`P00.14`, NIST TN 1297), for independent inputs,

**u_y² = Σ_j (∂f/∂x_j)² u_j²,**   (6)

and each term (∂f/∂x_j u_j)² is input j's share of the variance: the **uncertainty budget**. The sensitivities come from central differences on the reduction itself, so the formula needs no hand differentiation. A Monte Carlo check samples the inputs and pushes each sample through the reduction. Expanded uncertainty with coverage factor k = 2 (about 95 %) is 2u.

### 2.6 One point reduced by hand

The first run at nominal α = 4° in `SYNTHETIC_seed042_balance.csv` recorded α = 4.0184°, q = 740.66 Pa, N = 84.567 N, A = −5.0213 N and M_bal = +1.7727 N·m about the balance centre (x = 0.120 m, z = −0.020 m). The reference point is the quarter chord, (0.075, 0).

1. **Moment transfer, (4):** M_c/4 = 1.7727 + (0.075 − 0.120)(84.567) − (0 − (−0.020))(−5.0213) = 1.7727 − 3.8055 + 0.1004 = **−1.9324 N·m**. The 45 mm lever on the normal force is the big term; the 20 mm lever on the axial force is small but not negligible.
2. **Rotation, (2):** with cos α = 0.99754 and sin α = 0.07007, L = 84.567 × 0.99754 + 5.0213 × 0.07007 = **84.711 N**, D = 84.567 × 0.07007 − 5.0213 × 0.99754 = **0.917 N**. The drag is the small difference of two numbers of about 5.9 and 5.0 N, which is why it is fragile.
3. **Coefficients, (1):** qS = 740.66 × 0.180 = 133.32 N, so **c_l = 0.6354, c_d = 0.00688, c_m,c/4 = −1.9324/(133.32 × 0.30) = −0.0483**.

The code returns the same numbers (`main()` prints them with their uncertainties). The generator's truth at exactly 4° is c_l = 0.6388, c_d = 0.00711, c_m = −0.0487; the differences are the synthetic noise, and §4 shows they lie inside the stated uncertainty.

### 2.7 Where the model stops

- **Tares and interactions.** Real balances read the sting's own load and couple their channels; both are calibrated out before this lesson starts. Extension 1 and 2 add them.
- **Walls.** A model spanning a closed tunnel sees its own images in the walls: the measured c_l is a few per cent high (`P10.20`, `P10.22`).
- **Reynolds number.** Lift slope, zero-lift angle and a.c. are mostly set by the potential flow and transfer to the aeroplane with small corrections; drag and maximum lift are set by the boundary layer and do not (Problem 6, and `P10.06`).
- **Correlated errors.** Equation (6) assumes independence. A drifting q is correlated across points and makes a least-squares fit overconfident.

## 3. Build the computational model

Open `starter/main.py`. The synthetic generator and the CSV reader and writer are given, so the data exist before you write a line.

### Step 1: similarity parameters

`dynamic_pressure`, `reynolds_number`, `mach_number` are one line each. Accept arrays.

### Step 2: axes and moments

`body_to_wind` and `wind_to_body` are (2) and its inverse; `transfer_moment` is (4):

```python
def transfer_moment(M, N, A, x_from, z_from, x_to, z_to):
    return M + (x_to - x_from) * N - (z_to - z_from) * A
```

### Step 3: the reduction

`coefficients` is (1). `reduce_balance` chains them: transfer the moment from the balance centre to the reference point (quarter chord by default), rotate N, A to L, D with α in radians, divide. It also returns c_n and c_a, which the a.c. fit needs.

### Step 4: the aerodynamic centre

`fit_aerodynamic_centre(cn, cm, x_ref, c)` fits a straight line with `np.polyfit` and applies (5).

### Step 5: uncertainty

`propagate_uncertainty(func, x, u)` builds the Jacobian by central differences with a step proportional to the input's size and returns u_y and the budget. `monte_carlo_uncertainty` samples with `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)`.

### Adversarial self-checks

- **What if q doubles?** Every force doubles and every coefficient is unchanged (a test checks this); the relative effect of fixed balance noise halves, so u(c_d) from A and N halves while the α share stays.
- **What if V → 0?** q → 0 and every coefficient becomes noise divided by nothing: the reduction must refuse points below a minimum q. The home bench of `P10.23` runs at q ≈ 10 Pa, where this is the dominant problem.
- **Which assumption fails first?** Independence of errors: a drifting tunnel speed or a temperature-sensitive balance correlates the points, and the fit's ±0.0001c on x_ac becomes optimistic.
- **What measurement could falsify the reduction?** Run the same model inverted (α → −α, model upside down): a correct reduction gives c_l of opposite sign, the same c_d and an a.c. at the same place; a sign error in (4) shows up as an a.c. that moves.
- **Does the result survive a change of reference point or method?** Yes: four reference points give the same a.c. to 10⁻¹⁰ on clean data; Monte Carlo and first order agree to 1 %.
- **Which decision changes if the uncertainty doubles?** u(c_d) of 0.0014 (1σ) would make the drag difference between two candidate sections of 10 % undetectable; you would need a wake survey before choosing on drag.
