# Phase 03 Grading rubric

Used for the Phase 03 mini-project and for grading the exam's Part D. **The mini-project is the verification study from P03.18** (Verification & Validation): the method of manufactured solutions applied to the learner's *own* P03.11 finite-difference heat-equation solver or P03.12 finite-volume advection solver, with a grid-convergence study, Richardson extrapolation and a grid convergence index, a stated observed order against the theoretical one for every discretisation parameter, and a written verification report. It is chosen over the P03.16 wing MDO because it exercises what the phase is about, which is error: the learner must manufacture an exact solution (choose u, substitute it into the PDE, and move the residual into a source term), implement the source term and boundary data in a solver they wrote and whose bugs are their own, measure the discretisation error in stated norms over at least four nested grids, extract the observed order (lesson 18) and compare it with the von Neumann and truncation analysis they did in lessons 11 or 12, exercise the time-step and grid-spacing refinement separately and together (lessons 05, 06 and 13 for the order theory), check the solver's conservation and stability properties (lessons 12 and 14 for the linear algebra behind the implicit variants), and quantify the round-off floor (lesson 01) and the linear-solver tolerance (lessons 08 and 09) as competing error sources. It also produces the kind of document a real code must carry before anyone trusts its output, which the MDO project does not. The P03.16 wing aero-structural MDO is a strong second choice and may be substituted by a learner who documents a verified coupled-derivative (finite-difference, direct and adjoint agreement to a stated tolerance) and a convergence study of the multidisciplinary analysis; its verification and reproducibility requirements below then apply to the derivatives and the fixed-point iteration rather than to a PDE discretisation. Phase 03 has no physical experiment and no measured data, so the Phase 00 row on experiment design is replaced by a row on *verification design*, the Phase 02 data-provenance hard requirement is replaced by a **reproducibility** hard requirement: the repository must contain a single command (a script or a `make` target) that regenerates every number, table and figure in the report from the solver source, with the random seed, the grid sequence, the tolerances and the software versions recorded in the report, and the report must state that every result is a computation and that no physical measurement was made. Weights are proposed and will be calibrated in pilots. **Minimum thresholds apply in the mathematics and verification rows: presentation quality never compensates for a wrong error analysis, for an observed order that is quoted without the data behind it, or for a result that the code does not reproduce.**

| Dimension | Weight | 4 (exemplary) | 3 (proficient) | 2 (developing) | 1 (insufficient) | Minimum |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| Correct mathematics, derivation, assumptions | 25% | The discretisation's truncation error derived by Taylor expansion with its leading term and order in each parameter; the stability condition derived (von Neumann or eigenvalue analysis) and its consequence for how Δt and Δx must be refined together stated; the manufactured solution chosen with a reason (smooth, non-trivial in every term of the operator, non-zero at the boundaries so that boundary treatment is exercised) and the source term derived symbolically with a check that it reproduces the PDE residual to round-off; the error norms defined (discrete L², L∞, and the energy or conservation norm appropriate to the scheme) and the expected order in each stated before the runs | Truncation order and stability condition derived; manufactured solution and source term correct; norms defined; expected orders stated | A step asserted rather than derived, or the manufactured solution trivial for one term of the operator (e.g. linear in x for a diffusion operator), or the norm left undefined | Wrong truncation order, wrong stability condition, a source term that does not make the manufactured solution exact, or the theoretical order quoted from a lesson with no derivation | 3 |
| Calculation and numerical correctness | 20% | Solver, source term and norms implemented and tested; every number, table and figure in the report regenerated by the single reproducibility command; the manufactured solution satisfied to round-off when substituted into the discrete operator at the limit where it should be (e.g. a polynomial the scheme integrates exactly); the linear solves (if implicit) checked by residual to a tolerance stated and shown to be below the discretisation error on every grid; significant figures match the uncertainty; any seeded randomness recorded | Correct results, code runs and regenerates the report's numbers; solver tolerances stated | Results plausible but the code does not regenerate them, or a solver tolerance left at a default and never checked against the discretisation error | Arithmetic, unit, indexing (off-by-one at the boundary) or sign errors in the source term or the norms | 2 |
| Verification design and error accounting | 20% | At least four nested grids per refinement direction, with the refinement ratio stated; Δt and Δx refined separately and with the stability-preserving coupling (Δt ∝ Δx² for FTCS, Δt ∝ Δx for upwind) and the orders obtained in each case explained; the observed order computed from every consecutive triple, its approach to the theoretical value shown and the asymptotic range identified; the round-off floor located (the grid at which the error stops falling) and compared with the ε/h or εN model; the competing error sources (truncation, round-off, solver tolerance, boundary treatment) ranked by size with evidence; at least one deliberately injected error (a wrong stencil coefficient, a wrong boundary ghost value, a time argument bug) shown to be detected by the study | Four grids, observed order from every triple compared with theory, asymptotic range and floor identified, refinement coupling stated | Three grids or fewer, or the order computed from one pair only, or Δt and Δx refined together without saying what order to expect from that, or no floor sought | Order claimed from two grids, or the observed order quoted without the error table, or a study in which Δx is refined while a fixed Δt dominates the error unnoticed | 2 |
| Verification, validation, discrepancy investigation | 20% | Richardson extrapolation and GCI applied to a quantity without a known exact value (a flux, a total heat, an arrival time) and the extrapolate checked against the manufactured solution's value for it; conservation (Σu for the finite-volume solver, the discrete energy balance for the heat solver) checked to round-off on every grid; a limiting case (zero source, steady state, ν = 1 for upwind, r = 1/6 for FTCS) reproduced exactly; every discrepancy between observed and theoretical order either explained with evidence (a boundary treatment of lower order, a non-smooth manufactured solution, a tolerance floor) and fixed, or reported as an open issue with its size; the report says clearly that this is code verification, what solution verification would add, and what validation would require that this study cannot supply | Richardson and GCI applied and checked; conservation or a limiting case checked; discrepancies identified and plausibly explained; verification/validation distinction stated | Agreement with theory shown but no limiting case or conservation check, or a discrepancy noted but not pursued | Agreement claimed without a comparison, "the order looks about right", or an observed order of 1.1 against a theoretical 2 passed without comment | 2 |
| Communication, reproducibility, honesty about limits | 15% | Anyone could regenerate the entire report from the repository with one command, and the report states the command, the software versions, the seed and the grid sequence; every figure captioned with the norm, the grids and the theoretical order line; the report says what the study does not show (that the equations are the right model, that the code is correct for inputs outside the manufactured family, that performance is acceptable) and never presents a verified solver as a validated model | Clear report with code, command and limits stated; the report regenerates | Report incomplete or hard to follow; limits implicit; a number in the text not traceable to the code | Missing code, a figure that the code does not regenerate, a computed result presented as a measurement, or no statement that no physical measurement was made | Reproducibility command present and working (hard requirement) |

## Passing Phase 03

Pass requires: exam Parts A–D each at proficient level or above for the competencies they tag, **and** the mini-project at or above the minimum in every row with a weighted score of 3.0 or more. Report the outcome per competency as *practice completed*, *self-assessed mastery*, or *externally reviewed mastery* (the last only when a peer, mentor, or club reviewer has graded the report against this rubric). None of these is a credential.
