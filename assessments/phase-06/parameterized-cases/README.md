# Phase 06 parameterized validation cases

Unseen inputs for the computational problems, so answers cannot be copied. Each case is a JSON file `{"lesson": "P06.NN", "seed": int, "inputs": {...}, "expected": {...}, "tolerance": {...}}`. Generate new ones with `generate.py` (which uses the reference solutions) and check a learner's implementation with `run.py`. Expected values come from a closed form wherever the dynamics has one, and otherwise from an independent oracle: `scipy.integrate.solve_ivp` at tight tolerance on equations of motion written by separate code, a conservation law (energy, angular momentum, the inertial angular-momentum vector), an integration in a second frame (inertial against rotating), a second attitude representation (DCM against quaternion, both against `scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation`, which may appear in the oracle but never in the learner's code), `scipy.linalg.eigh` for generalised eigenproblems, `scipy.integrate.quad` for PSD integrals, and SymPy for derived equations of motion. Every case also contains a *limit* or a *conservation residual* that its dynamics must reproduce exactly or to a stated tolerance (zero damping, zero spin, zero rotation rate of the frame, infinite stiffness, small angle, an axisymmetric body, a symmetric linkage position), so an implementation that returns the right number at the generated point by tuning cannot pass. Tolerances are set so that a correct from-scratch implementation passes and a specific, named dynamics or convention error fails; each bullet states both, with the size of the error taken from a probe run on the exam's own numbers. Anything random inside a case (initial conditions, geometry, noise added to a synthetic record, a synthesized random signal) is generated from the case's seed with `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)`, and statistical tolerances are stated with the sample count or record length that justifies them. No case contains a measurement; inputs that imitate a measurement (a radar track, a pendulum swing record, a tap-test decay) are labelled `"synthetic": true` in the JSON, and `run.py` prints that label with the result.

Cases planned, one family per computational lesson:

- `p06-01-kinematics-*.json` (P06.01): a seeded analytic trajectory (polar, cylindrical or helical) or a sampled track of 50–500 points with optional seeded position noise → velocity and acceleration in Cartesian, polar, cylindrical and path components, speed, tangential and normal acceleration and radius of curvature at seeded instants. **Oracle:** symbolic differentiation of the analytic trajectory with SymPy, converted to each basis by separate code; for sampled tracks, the noise-free analytic values. **Limit:** a uniform circle about the origin gives a_t = 0 and ρ equal to its radius; straight-line flight gives a_n = 0 and an infinite ρ (reported as `inf`, not a division error). **Tolerance:** analytic cases rtol 10⁻⁹, so the polar Coriolis term 2ṙθ̇ written as ṙθ̇ (a_θ = −5.60 instead of −8.00 m/s² in the exam's question 5) and the radius of curvature computed with the horizontal instead of the full speed on a helix fail; sampled cases compare against the noise-free values within four times the propagated standard uncertainty √6σ/Δt² for accelerations, which a correct second difference meets with probability above 0.9999.
- `p06-02-pullup-*.json` (P06.02): mass, speed, load factor or radius, flight-path angle and bank angle from the seed → load factor or radius, lift, seat force, level-turn bank, radius and rate, and a `solve_ivp` point-mass path for a seeded n(t). **Oracle:** n = V²/(gR) + cos γ and the level-turn closed forms; the point-mass path integrated by independent code and its curvature from a circle fit. **Limit:** n = 1 gives an infinite pull-up radius; at γ = 90° the gravity term vanishes. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁹ on closed forms and 10⁻⁴ on the integrated radius, so the "1 +" omitted at the bottom of the pull-up (R = V²/(gn) = 440 m instead of 508 m) and the level-turn radius written with n instead of √(n² − 1) fail.
- `p06-03-arrest-*.json` (P06.03): aircraft mass, engagement speed and a force law (constant, F₀ + cv², or a tabulated cable–damper law with seeded coefficients), with optional thrust → stopping distance, time, peak deceleration, impulse and absorbed energy split. **Oracle:** the closed forms of the exam's question 7 where they exist; otherwise `solve_ivp` with a stopping event at rtol 10⁻¹¹, and the integrals ∫F dt and ∫F v dt by `quad` on the dense output. **Limit:** the impulse equals mV₀ and the work ½mV₀² for every law; F₀ = 0 must report "never stops" within a finite distance rather than return a number. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁶, so a work–energy integral taken over time instead of distance (which returns the impulse) and a damper force written as cv instead of cv² fail.
- `p06-04-rotating-frame-*.json` (P06.04): latitude, launch velocity relative to the ground, flight time or a ground-impact event, flat or spherical Earth → the Coriolis and centrifugal accelerations, the trajectory in the rotating frame, the deflection relative to a non-rotating Earth, and a Foucault precession rate. **Oracle:** the same trajectory integrated in an inertial frame (initial velocity v_rel + Ω × r) and rotated into Earth-fixed axes; the first-order flat-Earth formulas. **Limit:** Ω = 0 gives zero deflection; at the equator the Foucault rate is zero and at the pole it is Ω. **Tolerance:** frame-against-frame agreement 10⁻⁵ m over a 60 s flight (the exam's case agrees to 2 × 10⁻⁶ m), so a Coriolis term with the wrong sign or without its factor 2 (a 65 m error in the exam's east deflection) and an initial inertial velocity that omits Ω × r fail.
- `p06-05-four-bar-*.json` (P06.05): seeded link lengths (Grashof and non-Grashof), crank angle, rate and angular acceleration, and the assembly branch → classification, θ₃, θ₄, ω₃, ω₄, α₃, α₄, transmission angle, the coupler's instant centre and the rocker's limit positions. **Oracle:** Freudenstein's closed-form equation for θ₄; the velocity and acceleration closed forms; central differences of a separately written position solver. **Limit:** a parallelogram linkage gives ω₃ = 0 and ω₄ = ω₂; at a toggle position the velocity solve must report the singular Jacobian. **Tolerance:** angles atol 10⁻⁹ rad and rates rtol 10⁻⁸, so the crossed branch returned when the open one is asked for (θ₄ = 248.2° instead of 64.9° in the exam's question 9) and an acceleration that omits the centripetal terms rω² fail.
- `p06-06-wheel-*.json` (P06.06): wheel mass, radius, inertia, normal load, friction coefficient, touchdown speed and pre-rotation, with constant axle speed or a free wheel → spin-up time, slip distance, final spin, dissipated energy, axle work, and the free wheel's final speed. **Oracle:** the closed forms of the exam's question 10; a regularised-friction `solve_ivp` integration. **Limit:** dissipated energy equals the rotational energy gained for constant axle speed; the free wheel conserves angular momentum about the contact point; a solid disc ends at two thirds of its sliding speed. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁸ on closed forms and 10⁻⁴ on the integrated energy, so an energy balance that forgets the axle work (giving zero dissipation) and a free-wheel answer using momentum about the centre fail.
- `p06-07-attitude-dcm-*.json` (P06.07): a seeded Euler sequence (3-2-1, 3-1-3 or another) and angles, body rates, and a propagation span and step → the DCM, its orthonormality and determinant residuals, the recovered angles, the Euler-angle rates, the propagated DCM with and without re-orthonormalisation. **Oracle:** `Rotation.from_euler` (in the oracle only); the matrix exponential of the constant-rate rotation. **Limit:** zero rates leave C unchanged; at θ = ±90° the rate function must flag the singularity rather than return finite numbers. **Tolerance:** DCM entries atol 10⁻¹², so the elementary rotations multiplied in the wrong order and an active instead of a passive rotation (the transpose) fail; the propagated orthonormality error after re-orthonormalisation below 10⁻¹².
- `p06-08-quaternion-*.json` (P06.08): seeded attitudes as Euler angles or axis–angle, vectors to rotate, a second rotation in body or reference axes, a constant body rate, and SLERP fractions → quaternions (sign-normalised q₀ ≥ 0), rotated vectors, compositions, the DCM, the propagated quaternion and SLERP points. **Oracle:** the lesson 07 DCM path and `Rotation`; the closed-form constant-rate solution. **Limit:** q and −q give the same DCM; SLERP at t = 0 and 1 returns the end points and has constant angular rate. **Tolerance:** atol 10⁻¹², so a body-axis rotation composed on the wrong side (φ comes out wrong, not 100° in the exam's question 12), a JPL (scalar-last, left-handed) product mixed with a Hamilton one, and SLERP between q and −q taking the long way round fail.
- `p06-09-euler-equations-*.json` (P06.09): seeded principal inertias, initial rates (near each principal axis and generic) and a span → body rates and attitude over time, T and |H| residuals, the inertial H drift, wobble frequencies and the intermediate-axis growth rate. **Oracle:** the linearised frequencies and growth rate; the axisymmetric closed form; T, |H| and the inertial H vector as conservation residuals. **Limit:** spin exactly about a principal axis stays exactly there; an axisymmetric body's transverse rate rotates at λ = (I₃ − I₁)ω₃/I₁. **Tolerance:** relative conservation 10⁻⁸ over 200 s and the inertial H within 10⁻⁷ of |H| (the reference meets them with margins of a hundred or more), so an error in the attitude kinematics, which conserves T and |H| exactly, fails the inertial check, and a sign error in Euler's equations fails the axisymmetric case.
- `p06-10-spin-stability-*.json` (P06.10): seeded spin and transverse inertias (minor- and major-axis spinners), spin rate, a dissipation power or a damper model, and a body-fixed or inertially fixed torque → H, energies, flat-spin rate, nutation and coning rates, the direction of nutation-angle drift, and the H deviation under torque. **Oracle:** T(θ) at fixed H; the torqued Euler equations with quaternion attitude integrated independently. **Limit:** I_s = I_t gives no nutation drift; zero torque gives zero H deviation. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁹ on closed forms and 10⁻³ on the simulated H deviation (the exam's case gives 0.4838° against 2τ/(Hω_s) = 0.4838°), so a precession computed with the inertially fixed torque formula for a body-fixed torque (15.2° instead of 0.48°) fails.
- `p06-11-lagrange-*.json` (P06.11): a seeded double pendulum (masses, lengths) or a pendulum on a cart, with initial conditions → equations of motion, linear natural frequencies and mode shapes, nonlinear trajectories, energy residuals, and the link tensions. **Oracle:** the equations derived again by SymPy in the oracle; a Newton–Euler evaluation of the accelerations and tensions at seeded states; `eigh` of the linearised M and K. **Limit:** equal masses and lengths give ω² = (g/l)(2 ∓ √2); m₂ → 0 gives a single pendulum of length l₁. **Tolerance:** Newton–Euler residuals below 10⁻⁹ of the weight, linear frequencies rtol 10⁻¹⁰, and energy drift below 10⁻⁷ of the swing energy over the integration span, so a missing cos(α − β) coupling or a velocity-squared term with the wrong sign fails at once.
- `p06-12-hamiltonian-*.json` (P06.12, ★): a seeded pendulum energy or a Kepler orbit (radius, eccentricity), a step size and an integrator choice → Hamilton's equations' trajectory, phase-space area of an orbit, energy error band and drift, and the area-preservation residual of one step map. **Oracle:** the exact pendulum period by the complete elliptic integral and the action by `quad`; Kepler's elements and closed-form period; the Jacobian determinant of the step map by finite differences. **Limit:** a symplectic step has determinant 1 to round-off; explicit Euler on a harmonic oscillator has 1 + h²ω². **Tolerance:** determinant atol 10⁻¹² for symplectic maps, so a "symplectic Euler" that updates q and p from the old values (explicit Euler) fails; relative energy error of leapfrog within 1.1(hω)²/4 (the reference reaches (hω)²/4 at h = 0.1).
- `p06-13-sdof-*.json` (P06.13): seeded m, k, ζ (0 to 0.7), force amplitude and frequency or base motion, and a synthetic free-decay record (`"synthetic": true`) with seeded noise → ω_n, ω_d, ζ, δ, the frequency response and transmissibility at seeded frequencies, and ζ identified from the record. **Oracle:** the closed forms; a `solve_ivp` steady state after the transient has decayed below 10⁻¹². **Limit:** ζ = 0 gives an unbounded response at r = 1 (the function must say so); r → 0 gives the static deflection and T → 1. **Tolerance:** closed forms rtol 10⁻¹⁰, so a decrement not divided by the number of cycles (ζ = 0.50 instead of 0.050 in the exam's question 25, item 4) and a force transmissibility used for base excitation (the same formula, a different meaning, flagged by the label) fail; the identified ζ within four standard errors of the generating value from 1000 seeded replicates.
- `p06-14-modes-*.json` (P06.14): a seeded chain or a random symmetric positive-definite M and K of size 2–8, initial conditions, a harmonic or base forcing, and two damping ratios → frequencies, mass-normalised modes, participation factors and effective masses, modal and direct free and forced responses, Rayleigh coefficients. **Oracle:** `scipy.linalg.eigh(K, M)`; direct integration of M ẍ + C ẋ + K x = F at rtol 10⁻¹²; the chain's closed form. **Limit:** effective masses sum to the total mass; a rigid-body mode (an unrestrained chain) has ω = 0. **Tolerance:** frequencies rtol 10⁻¹⁰, modes compared up to sign at atol 10⁻⁹, modal against direct response atol 10⁻⁸ of the initial amplitude, so modes normalised to unit length instead of unit modal mass and a modal superposition that ignores the initial velocities fail.
- `p06-15-beam-modes-*.json` (P06.15): seeded EI, mass per length, length, boundary conditions (cantilever, pinned–pinned, free–free, clamped–clamped), an optional tip mass, and element counts → frequency-equation roots, natural frequencies, mode shapes at seeded stations, and the learner's finite-difference or finite-element frequencies on two meshes. **Oracle:** `brentq` on each frequency equation; a Hermite-cubic consistent-mass model in the oracle on a fine mesh. **Limit:** the tip-mass equation with zero tip mass reduces to cos βL cosh βL = −1; mode shapes satisfy the boundary conditions to 10⁻¹⁰. **Tolerance:** closed-form frequencies rtol 10⁻¹⁰; finite-element frequencies above the exact ones, with an observed order 4 ± 0.3 for Hermite elements (the exam's question 19 gives ratios 14.8 and 15.7), so a lumped-mass model reported as consistent and a pinned boundary condition imposed on the slope fail.
- `p06-16-random-vib-*.json` (P06.16): a seeded piecewise log–log input PSD (3–6 breakpoints, slopes of −6 to +6 dB/octave), one or two modes with Q from 5 to 50, and a record length and seed for synthesis → input Grms, Miles' response, the full ∫|H|²S response, relative-displacement rms, and the time-domain response rms of a synthesized `"synthetic": true` record. **Oracle:** the analytic segment integrals (with the n = −1 logarithmic case) and `quad`; the time-domain rms against the analytic value. **Limit:** a flat PSD gives Grms = √(S Δf); Q → ∞ makes Miles and the full integral agree to the out-of-band contribution. **Tolerance:** analytic integrals rtol 10⁻⁹, so the logarithmic case mishandled at −3 dB/octave (a division by zero or a wrong area) and Miles evaluated with f_n in rad/s (a factor √(2π), 28.1 instead of 11.2 g in the exam) fail; the time-domain rms within 3% of the analytic value for a 60 s record, about 2.6 standard deviations of a sample rms (1.15%) when the response's effective bandwidth is πf_n/(2Q) (31 Hz for the exam's mode, about 1900 independent samples of the mean square).
- `p06-17-isolation-*.json` (P06.17): component mass, target natural frequency or stiffness, damping ratios, the spectrum of a P06.16 case, and a static acceleration → stiffness per mount, transmissibility at seeded frequencies, isolated response Grms, relative-displacement rms and 3σ sway space including static sag. **Oracle:** the closed-form transmissibility and `quad` of |H|²S on a separately written spectrum. **Limit:** T = 1 at √2 f_n for every ζ; ζ → 0 gives T ~ 1/r² far above resonance. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁹ on closed forms and 10⁻⁶ on integrals, so an isolator check that only compares T at one frequency (passing the exam's 300 Hz mounts) fails the integrated-response case, and a sway space without the static sag fails.
- `p06-18-landing-gear-*.json` (P06.18): seeded sprung and unsprung masses, sink speed, lift fraction, gas spring (area, pressure, column length, exponent), orifice coefficient and tyre stiffness → peak ground force and load factor, maximum stroke and tyre deflection, energy terms at maximum compression, strut efficiency and the energy residual. **Oracle:** the same model integrated by independent code with event handling for top-out and tyre lift-off; the energy balance as a residual. **Limit:** zero orifice damping conserves energy to the integration tolerance and rebounds to the drop speed; a rigid strut with lift equal to weight reduces to a mass on a tyre spring with n = v√(k_t/m)/g. **Tolerance:** energy residual below 10⁻⁸ of the drop energy and peak loads rtol 10⁻³, so a tyre that can pull (no lift-off constraint), a damping force written as c_dṡ² without the sign of ṡ, and a missing wheel-weight term (0.027 kJ in the exam's case, far above the residual tolerance) fail.

These are written once the reference solutions for lessons 01–18 are merged; `generate.py` then produces them deterministically from a seed. The lesson 19 pendulum reduction (period against amplitude, the decrement-against-amplitude damping test and the windowed least-squares fits) is covered by the exam's question 24 and by the lesson's own tests against `make_synthetic_swing`, and needs no seeded case, since its unseen input is the learner's own swing record.
