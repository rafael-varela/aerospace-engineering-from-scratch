# Phase 05 Grading rubric

Used for the Phase 05 mini-project and for grading the exam's Part D. **The mini-project is the cantilever report from P05.18** (Home Lab: Cantilever Beam Deflection, `projects/p05-cantilever`): the deflection of a clamped strip measured against load, and along its length, compared with a prediction written down and dated before the first load. It is the only physical experiment of the phase, and it exercises the whole of what the phase is about, which is equilibrium, compatibility and constitutive law with every result checked by an independent route: the learner draws the free-body diagram and finds the bending moment by equilibrium (lessons 01, 02 and 09), computes I from the measured section (lesson 05), uses Hooke's law and the flexure relation (lessons 06 and 10), integrates EI v'' = M with the clamped boundary conditions (compatibility, lesson 12) and checks it by Castigliano or a finite-difference solution (lessons 12 and 16), checks that the root stress stays far below yield (lesson 14), predicts with an uncertainty budget (`P00.14`) in which the thickness enters cubed, measures load–deflection with loading and unloading, fits the compliance with both Type A and Type B terms, compares by normalised error, and must explain a discrepancy that is usually a boundary-condition error (a compliant clamp, an effective root inside the clamp) or a thickness error, rather than a fault in beam theory. A second free length separates a length offset (whose effect grows as L shrinks) from an error in E or t (a constant factor), and the ratio of compliances between two lengths and the deflected shape along the strip test the beam model independently of E and t, so a good report contains a real model test, not only a fitted modulus. Lesson 17's CAD drawing is graded through its own tests and the exam's question 20, not here, because it involves no measurement. A learner without a kit completes the mini-project with the synthetic dataset supplied in the lesson, labels it synthetic everywhere it appears, and is graded on the same rows except that the experiment-design row grades the *design* (prediction, procedure, uncertainty budget) and the report must say that no physical measurement was made; such a report is recorded as *practice completed*, never as a measured result.

**Hard requirements for the cantilever report** (and for the exam's Part D question 22 when answered with the learner's own data):

1. **Safety box completed.** The report contains the project's safety box (`projects/p05-cantilever/safety.md`, in the format of `projects/p04-pendulum/safety.md`: hazards, energy limits, controls, supervision, location, regulations, stop criteria, disposal, not permitted), filled in for the setup actually used, with any deviation from the procedure stated. **A report without a completed safety box is not graded**, whatever else it contains. The box must record: total hanging mass ≤ 2 kg; no loading of glass, carbon-fibre rods or anything that can splinter; safety glasses worn when loading strips that can snap; the clamp checked before each load; nothing hung over feet; and loading stopped at visible permanent set or cracking.
2. **Prediction dated before measurement.** `data/prediction.md` (or the equivalent) holds the predicted compliance or tip deflection for each free length, from measured b, t and L and a stated E, each with its uncertainty, and the date; that date precedes the first raw-data file. A prediction written or revised after the data were seen is reported as a post-hoc fit and graded as no prediction (row 3 at most *developing*).
3. **Uncertainty reported.** Every measured and every predicted number carries a standard uncertainty with its type (A, B or combined) and the coverage factor if expanded; every comparison is a normalised error, not "close". A number without an uncertainty is graded *insufficient* in row 2.
4. **Measurement, computation and synthetic data kept apart.** Raw readings are kept unedited, including the loading and unloading sequence and every zero reading; every value computed by a model, a fit or a simulation (the predicted compliance, the fitted E, a finite-difference profile) is labelled as computed; synthetic data are labelled synthetic in every table, figure and caption. Presenting a calculation or a synthetic dataset as a measurement fails row 5 outright.

Weights are proposed and will be calibrated in pilots. **Minimum thresholds apply in the mechanics, experiment and safety rows: presentation quality never compensates for wrong mechanics, an undated prediction, a missing uncertainty or an unsafe method.**

## Grading Part D of the exam

Questions 22 and 23 are graded on the same rows, scaled to a question: row 2 (the fit with both uncertainty types and a correct E_n; in 23, every margin recomputed with the right factor and criterion), row 4 (ranked causes using the length dependence, a discriminating follow-up with predicted outcomes, and the two tests that are independent of E and t; in 23, the missing buckling mode found and the correct item left alone), and row 5 (honest conclusions, measured versus predicted versus synthetic kept apart, and the hard requirements and safety limits named). A learner who answers question 22 with their own `p05-cantilever` data attaches the dated prediction file and the completed safety box; without them the answer is graded as if the synthetic table had been used, and the own-data claim is not recorded.

## Passing Phase 05

Pass requires: exam Parts A–D each at proficient level or above for the competencies they tag, **and** the mini-project at or above the minimum in every row with a weighted score of 3.0 or more. Report the outcome per competency as *practice completed*, *self-assessed mastery*, or *externally reviewed mastery* (the last only when a peer, mentor, or club reviewer has graded the report against this rubric). A mini-project completed with synthetic data only is recorded as *practice completed* for competency 4 until a measured report is graded. None of these is a credential, and nothing in this phase certifies anyone to analyse or sign off real aircraft structure.
