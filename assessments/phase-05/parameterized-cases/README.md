# Phase 05 parameterized validation cases

Unseen inputs for the computational problems, so answers cannot be copied. Each case is a JSON file `{"lesson": "P05.NN", "seed": int, "inputs": {...}, "expected": {...}, "tolerance": {...}}`. Generate new ones with `generate.py` (which uses the reference solutions) and check a learner's implementation with `run.py`. Expected values come from a closed form wherever the mechanics has one, and otherwise from an independent oracle: `numpy.linalg.solve` and `numpy.linalg.matrix_rank` on an equilibrium matrix assembled by separate code, the polygon (Green's theorem) formulas for section properties, `numpy.linalg.eigh` for principal values, `scipy.integrate.quad` for energy and load integrals, and exact rational arithmetic (`fractions.Fraction`) for singularity-function diagrams. Every case also contains a *limit* or an *equilibrium residual* that its mechanics must reproduce exactly (zero load, a symmetric structure under a symmetric load, a section with an axis of symmetry, a free end with zero moment, a uniaxial stress state, an infinitely stiff member), so an implementation that returns the right number at the generated point by tuning cannot pass. Tolerances are set so that a correct from-scratch implementation passes and a specific, named mechanics or convention error fails; each bullet states both, with the size of the error taken from a probe run on the exam's own numbers. Anything random inside a case (geometry, loads, noise added to a synthetic test record, Monte Carlo samples) is generated from the case's seed with `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)`, and statistical tolerances are stated with the sample count that justifies them. No case contains a measurement; inputs that imitate a measurement (a tensile record, a load–deflection table) are labelled `"synthetic": true` in the JSON, and `run.py` prints that label with the result.

Cases planned, one family per computational lesson:

- `p05-02-reactions-*.json` (P05.02): a rigid body in 2-D or 3-D from the seed, with 1–6 applied forces and couples at random points and a support set drawn from pins, rollers, fixed supports and ball joints (including deliberately deficient sets: all reactions parallel, or all concurrent) → the reaction components, a determinacy verdict (determinate, indeterminate of degree k, or improperly constrained) and the equilibrium residual. **Oracle:** the equilibrium matrix assembled independently and solved with `numpy.linalg.solve`; the verdict from `numpy.linalg.matrix_rank` against the number of unknowns and of equations (3 or 6). **Limit:** zero applied load gives zero reactions exactly; a load through a single pin gives a reaction equal and opposite to it. **Tolerance:** reactions rtol 10⁻⁹ against the oracle and ΣF, ΣM residuals below 10⁻⁹ of the largest load times the largest lever arm, so a moment computed as F × r instead of r × F (every moment's sign flipped) fails by the order of the loads; the verdict must match exactly, so an implementation that counts unknowns but never checks the rank, and therefore calls three parallel rollers "determinate", fails the deficient cases.
- `p05-03-truss-*.json` (P05.03): a seeded planar truss (Pratt, Howe or Warren panels, 2–12 panels, or a random triangulation with 5–25 joints), supports, and joint loads → every member force, the reactions, the method-of-sections forces in three members cut by a seeded section, a stability verdict, and the joint displacements for given EA. **Oracle:** an independent assembly of the joint-equilibrium matrix solved with `numpy.linalg.solve`; the three cut members by the moment equations about the section's two intersection points (the exam's question 6 is the template); the displacements by the unit-load sum ΣFfL/(AE) at each loaded joint. **Limit:** a symmetric truss under a symmetric load gives symmetric member forces to 10⁻¹² relative; zero load gives zero forces; a case with one diagonal removed is a mechanism and must be reported as such (rank deficient), not solved. **Tolerance:** member forces rtol 10⁻⁹ with an absolute floor of 10⁻⁹ of the largest load (zero-force members must come out as zero to that floor); the joint residuals below 10⁻⁹ of the largest load; so a direction cosine taken from the wrong end (a member's force applied to both joints with the same sign) fails every residual, and a tension/compression sign convention that differs from the stated one fails the sign of every member.
- `p05-05-section-properties-*.json` (P05.05): a seeded section built from 2–5 rectangles (I, T, channel, Z, angle, hat) or a simple polygon of 4–16 vertices given in either orientation → area, centroid, I_x, I_y, I_xy about centroidal axes, principal moments and the principal angle. **Oracle:** the polygon formulas (the exam's question 8(b)) on the outline, and the composite parallel-axis sum on the rectangles, as two routes that must agree; principal values from `numpy.linalg.eigh` of [[I_x, −I_xy], [−I_xy, I_y]]. **Limit:** a section with a vertical axis of symmetry has x̄ on it and I_xy = 0 to 10⁻¹² of I_x; a single rectangle gives bh³/12 exactly; reversing the vertex order must not change any output. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻¹⁰ on every property and atol 10⁻⁸ degrees on the angle (unique within (−90°, 90°], the major axis reported), so leaving out the parallel-axis terms (17% of the true I_x for the exam's I-section), the 1/2 in tan 2θ_p (42.9° instead of 21.4° for the exam's angle), a sign error on I_xy (−21.4°), and a clockwise polygon returning a negative area all fail.
- `p05-06-stress-strain-*.json` (P05.06): a synthetic tensile record (`"synthetic": true`) generated from a Ramberg–Osgood curve ε = σ/E + 0.002(σ/σ₀.₂)ⁿ with E, σ₀.₂ and n from the seed (aluminium, titanium or steel ranges), sampled at 500–2000 points up to a seeded ultimate strength, with Gaussian noise of 2 microstrain on strain and 0.5 MPa on stress, and the coupon's dimensions → modulus, 0.2% offset yield, ultimate strength, uniform elongation, modulus of resilience and the area under the curve to maximum load. **Oracle:** the generating parameters (by construction the offset yield of a Ramberg–Osgood curve is exactly σ₀.₂, and E is exact); the area by `quad` on the noise-free curve. **Limit:** with n → ∞ (an elastic–perfectly plastic record) the offset yield equals the plateau stress. **Tolerance:** E rtol 0.5% and yield rtol 0.3%: the noise alone gives a standard deviation of 0.04% in E for a fit over the points below 0.5σ₀.₂ (2000 seeded replicates of a 719-point fit), and a fit window that is too wide fails, since fitting E up to 0.9σ₀.₂ lowers E by 4.6% and raises the yield by 0.8% on the exam's 2024-T3 parameters; a 0.2% offset entered as 0.2 instead of 0.002 never intersects the curve and must raise an error rather than return a number.
- `p05-07-bolted-joint-*.json` (P05.07): bolt and member materials, bolt diameter, grip, equivalent member area, preload, an external load history and a temperature change from the seed → stiffnesses, joint factor C, bolt and clamp loads, separating load, and preload after the temperature change. **Oracle:** the closed forms of the exam's question 10, with the thermal change as mismatch strain × grip × series stiffness. **Limit:** an infinitely stiff member gives C = 0 (the bolt load does not change with P until separation); identical bolt and member expansion coefficients give no thermal change; above the separating load the bolt carries all of P. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻¹⁰, so C and (1 − C) swapped (bolt load 12.1 kN instead of 8.90 kN in the exam's joint), springs combined in parallel instead of series for the thermal term, and a missing change of regime at separation all fail.
- `p05-08-torsion-*.json` (P05.08): power and speed or a torque, a solid or hollow circular shaft with a diameter ratio, an allowable shear stress and G; plus a single-cell or two-cell thin-walled closed section given by its wall midline polygon and thicknesses → torque, diameter, twist, shear flow and stress in each wall, rate of twist, torsion constant and the open-section comparison. **Oracle:** the closed forms for circular shafts; for one cell, Bredt–Batho; for two cells, the 3 × 3 linear system (two shear flows and the common twist rate) solved with `numpy.linalg.solve`. **Limit:** k = 0 reproduces the solid shaft; a two-cell section whose internal wall is made vanishingly thin reproduces the single cell of its outer outline. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻¹⁰, so radius used in place of diameter in 16T/(πd³) (a factor 8 in stress), rpm used as rad/s (a factor 9.55 in torque), A in place of 2A in Bredt–Batho (a factor 2 in shear flow) and A² in place of 4A² in the twist rate (a factor 4) all fail.
- `p05-09-shear-moment-*.json` (P05.09): a simply supported, cantilever or overhanging beam with 1–6 point loads, 1–3 distributed loads (uniform or linearly varying over seeded intervals) and 0–2 concentrated couples → reactions, V and M at seeded stations (with left and right values at every discontinuity), the extreme moments and their locations, and the points of contraflexure. **Oracle:** the singularity-function expressions integrated in exact rational arithmetic with `fractions.Fraction` for rational inputs; the extremes by solving V = 0 in each segment and comparing with the values at every jump and support. **Limit:** M = 0 at every pin, roller and free end; V and M at the far end close to zero (the global equilibrium check); a single central point load on a simple span gives PL/4. **Tolerance:** atol 10⁻⁹ of the largest |M| at every station, so a couple applied with the wrong sign (in the exam's question 12 it changes R_B from 7.50 to 6.50 kN and moves the largest sagging moment from 2.00 kN m at x = 1⁺ to 1.50 kN m at x = 1⁻), a distributed load not "switched off" at its end, and an extreme found only where V = 0 (missing the jump at a couple) all fail.
- `p05-10-unsymmetric-bending-*.json` (P05.10): a seeded open thin-walled section (angle, Z, channel, or a 4–8 vertex polygon) with moments M_x and M_y → the stress at every vertex, the maximum tensile and compressive stress, and the neutral-axis angle. **Oracle:** the general formula with I_xy (the exam's question 13(d)) on properties computed by the polygon oracle, and independently the stress from a rotation to principal axes. **Limit:** a section with I_xy = 0 and M_y = 0 reduces to −M_xy/I_x exactly; zero moments give zero stress. **Tolerance:** atol 10⁻⁹ of the largest stress, so ignoring I_xy (a factor 2.2 at the heel of the exam's angle, and the wrong sign at the tip of its horizontal leg) fails at once.
- `p05-11-shear-flow-*.json` (P05.11): a thin-walled I, channel, Z or hat section with seeded dimensions and a vertical shear → the shear flow at seeded points along the wall, the maximum shear stress, the resultant in each wall, and the shear-centre position. **Oracle:** q = VQ/I evaluated in closed form wall by wall; the shear centre from the moment of the wall resultants, checked for a channel against e = 3b²t_f/(6bt_f + ht_w). **Limit:** the web resultants sum to V and the flange resultants of a symmetric section cancel, to 10⁻¹² relative; a doubly symmetric section has its shear centre at the centroid. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁹, so Q taken about the bottom face instead of the neutral axis, and a shear centre computed with an I that omits the web (which gives e = b/2, 20.0 mm instead of 14.1 mm for the exam's channel) fail.
- `p05-12-deflection-*.json` (P05.12): a cantilever, simply supported or propped beam with seeded point, uniform and linearly varying loads and EI → deflection and slope at seeded stations by integration and superposition, plus the learner's finite-difference solution on two grids. **Oracle:** closed-form superposition of the standard cases; `scipy.integrate.quad` of the moment-area integrals as a second route. **Limit:** EI → ∞ gives zero deflection; the propped cantilever's prop force is 3wL/8 under uniform load. **Tolerance:** closed-form outputs rtol 10⁻¹⁰; the finite-difference tip deflection within 2 × 10⁻³ relative at n = 40 and its error ratio between n and 2n equal to 4 ± 0.2 (the exam's question 15 gives 3.99 and 4.00), so a first-order clamp condition (v₁ = v₀ instead of a ghost node) is caught by the order test, and a deflection that is half the right size (a missing factor in EI v'' = M) fails the closed form.
- `p05-13-mohr-*.json` (P05.13): a seeded 2-D stress state and a seeded symmetric 3-D stress tensor, and a list of plane orientations → principal stresses and directions, maximum in-plane and absolute maximum shear, and normal and shear stress on each plane. **Oracle:** `numpy.linalg.eigh` for principal values and vectors; the traction t = σn on each plane. **Limit:** a uniaxial state gives σ₁ = σ and τ_max = σ/2; a hydrostatic state gives zero shear on every plane; pure shear gives principal axes at 45°. **Tolerance:** atol 10⁻⁹ of the largest stress and atol 10⁻⁸ degrees on directions (compared modulo 180°), so θ_p without the 1/2 (49.3° instead of 24.7° in the exam's fuselage skin) fails, and so does reporting the in-plane maximum shear as the absolute maximum when both in-plane principal stresses have the same sign (26.4 instead of 39.0 MPa there).
- `p05-14-margins-*.json` (P05.14): a seeded combined stress state (from bending, torsion, axial and pressure terms), a material from the phase's table, a load type (limit), a fitting flag, and for brittle cases tensile and compressive strengths → von Mises and Tresca equivalent stresses, margins at yield and at ultimate with the factors 1.0 and 1.5 (and 1.15 for fittings at ultimate), and the Mohr–Coulomb factor of safety. **Oracle:** principal stresses by `eigh`, then the criteria and the margin definitions of the exam preamble. **Limit:** uniaxial stress gives equal von Mises and Tresca; pure shear gives a Tresca/von Mises ratio of 2/√3; a zero stress state returns an infinite margin, not a division error. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻¹⁰ on every margin, so the 1.5 forgotten at ultimate (+0.53 instead of +0.02 in the exam's question 23, item 1), the 1.5 applied at yield, the fitting factor applied at yield, and a maximum-principal-stress criterion used for a ductile metal all fail.
- `p05-15-buckling-*.json` (P05.15): a seeded tube, rod or rectangular section, length, end condition (K = 2, 1, 0.699, 0.5) and material → slenderness, transition slenderness, Euler and Johnson stresses, the governing critical load, and the learner's finite-difference critical load for pinned–pinned and fixed–free ends on a stated grid. **Oracle:** the closed forms; the discrete eigenvalue (4/h²)sin²(πh/(2L)) for the pinned–pinned grid as the exact answer for the finite-difference case. **Limit:** at the transition slenderness Euler and Johnson agree to 10⁻¹²; fixed–free at L equals pinned–pinned at 2L. **Tolerance:** closed forms rtol 10⁻¹⁰, so K applied to L but not squared (a factor 2 instead of 4 for fixed–free) and the Euler formula used below the transition (49% unconservative at L/r = 58 in the exam's question 18) fail; the finite-difference load rtol 10⁻⁴ against Euler at n = 200 (the discretisation error there is 2.1 × 10⁻⁵) and it must lie below the exact load.
- `p05-16-castigliano-*.json` (P05.16): a seeded statically determinate truss, a cantilever or simple beam with several loads, a bent rod in bending and torsion (the exam's question 19(d) family), or a once-redundant beam or truss → the deflection at seeded points by Castigliano and by the unit-load method, the strain energy, and the redundant force. **Oracle:** `quad` on the energy integrands with the internal forces written independently; ΣFfL/(AE) for trusses; the redundant from compatibility checked against the closed form where one exists (3wL/8 for the propped cantilever). **Limit:** ½Pδ equals the strain energy for a single load; Maxwell–Betti symmetry of the flexibility matrix to 10⁻¹². **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁸, so a missing torsion term (which leaves 40% of the true deflection in the exam's bent rod), a missing axial term in a truss member, and a unit-load solution with the unit load at the wrong joint all fail.
- `p05-17-drawing-check-*.json` (P05.17): a seeded bracket profile (outline plus holes and slots), a dimension scheme, a thickness and a material, and a chain of 3–8 toleranced dimensions → area, mass, centroid, the constraint verdict of the dimension scheme (fully constrained, under-constrained with the free degrees of freedom, or over-constrained with the redundant dimension), worst-case and RSS stack-up, and a Monte Carlo estimate of the fraction outside a requirement with normal and with uniform spreads. **Oracle:** the polygon formulas for mass properties; the rank of the Jacobian of the dimension equations with respect to the vertex coordinates for the constraint verdict; exact sums for worst case and RSS; for the Monte Carlo, the analytic normal probability where it applies. **Limit:** a single toleranced dimension gives identical worst-case and RSS results; a scheme with one dimension removed must be reported as one degree of freedom short. **Tolerance:** mass properties rtol 10⁻¹⁰; stack-ups exact to 10⁻¹²; the Monte Carlo standard deviation within 1% of the analytic value with 10⁵ samples (four standard errors of a sample standard deviation is 0.9% at that size) and the out-of-requirement fraction within four binomial standard deviations, √(p(1 − p)/N), of the analytic or reference value (a correct implementation fails one such check with probability 6 × 10⁻⁵); so RSS computed on worst-case sums, a tolerance treated as 1σ instead of 3σ, and a uniform spread sampled as normal all fail.

These are written once the reference solutions for lessons 02, 03, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 17 are merged; `generate.py` then produces them deterministically from a seed. Lesson 01 (forces, moments and free-body diagrams) is assessed through its own tests and the exam's questions 1 and 5, since its build is a special case of the lesson 02 reaction solver; lesson 04 (frames, machines and friction) through its tests and question 7, because its outputs (mechanical advantage, the friction-circle iteration, the capstan ratio, the jackscrew torques) are closed forms already checked there; and lesson 18 through its physical report, which the rubric grades. The cantilever lab's reduction (the compliance fit, the cube-root offset fit and the shape test) is covered by the exam's question 22 and needs no seeded case, since its unseen input is the learner's own strip.
