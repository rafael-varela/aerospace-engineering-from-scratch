# Phase 09 Grading rubric

Used for the Phase 09 mini-project and for grading the exam's Part D. **The mini-project is the servo feedback report from P09.21** (Home Lab: Servo Feedback Control, `projects/p09-servo-control`): a small actuator, either a hobby servo with its feedback wiper brought out (Option A) or a gear motor with a driver and a potentiometer (Option B), identified from open-loop step tests, given a P or PI controller designed to a stated target, and then run in closed loop, with the predicted rise time, overshoot and settling time written down and dated before the loop was first closed. It is the only physical experiment of the phase, and it exercises the whole of what the phase is about, which is *model, analyse, design, verify* with every result checked by an independent route. The learner writes the plant model and its assumptions (lessons 03, 04 and 13), identifies it from step responses with standard errors (lesson 21, building on `P00.14` and `P03.14`), designs the gain from the closed-loop pole locations and the step-response formulas (lessons 04, 08 and 11), predicts the metrics with an uncertainty, closes the loop on a microcontroller that samples, delays and holds its output (lessons 06, 07 and 17), and must then explain a discrepancy. The discrepancy is usually the loop delay (one sample of firmware delay plus the hold), a dead zone, a duty or slew limit, or quantisation, rather than a fault in the linear model, and the lesson's refined models (the delay as a lag, and the full sampled simulation) test that explanation. A second loop rate or a second step size separates a delay (whose effect grows as the loop slows) or a saturation (whose effect grows with the step) from an error in K or τ (a fixed shift), so a good report contains a real test of the explanation, not only a refitted gain. Margins, robustness and the other design lessons are graded through their own tests and the exam's Parts B and C, not here, because they involve no measurement. A learner without a kit completes the mini-project with the synthetic dataset in `projects/p09-servo-control/data/synthetic/`, labels it synthetic everywhere it appears, writes the dated prediction from the synthetic open-loop files before opening the closed-loop ones, and is graded on the same rows except that the experiment-design row grades the *design* (prediction, procedure, safety tests planned, uncertainty budget). That report must say that no physical measurement was made; it is recorded as *practice completed*, never as a measured result.

**Hard requirements for the servo report** (and for the exam's Part D question 23 when answered with the learner's own data):

1. **Safety box completed and pre-run tests logged.** The report contains the project's safety box (`projects/p09-servo-control/safety.md`, in the format of `projects/p04-electronics-bench/safety.md`: hazards, energy limits, controls, supervision, location, regulations, stop criteria, disposal, not permitted), filled in for the setup actually used, with any deviation from the procedure stated, **and** the dated log of the four firmware tests (stop, limit, sign, travel limit) for every session, run before the first closed loop. **A report without a completed safety box and the test log is not graded**, whatever else it contains. The box must record: battery or USB power only, never mains; the motor pack measured at ≤ 6.0 V before every session (never four fresh alkaline cells); one motor or micro servo with stall current ≤ 500 mA and a 0.5 A resettable fuse in the pack's + lead; nothing on the shaft except a light pointer (≤ 5 g, ≤ 80 mm), with no propellers, fans, blades or wheels; the software duty limit (|u| ≤ 0.6) or command limit (±60°), the travel limit and the stop button, none of them removed or raised; fingers, hair and sleeves clear while the loop runs; the first closed loop at half gain with a small step; and the stop criteria respected.
2. **Prediction dated before the loop is closed.** `data/prediction.md` (or the equivalent) holds the identified parameters with their uncertainties, the designed gain(s), and for each planned step the predicted rise time, overshoot, settling time, steady-state error and peak duty or command, with the date and time; that date precedes the first closed-loop raw file. The redesign's prediction is dated before its run. A prediction written or revised after the closed-loop data were seen is reported as a post-hoc fit and graded as no prediction (row 3 at most *developing*).
3. **Uncertainty reported.** Every identified parameter carries a standard error from the fit and a spread over repeats; every measured metric carries its spread over at least three repeats; every predicted metric carries an uncertainty propagated from the identification; every comparison is a normalised error (or a stated tolerance such as "within 15 % and 3 percentage points"), not "close". A number without an uncertainty is graded *insufficient* in row 2.
4. **Measurement, computation and synthetic data kept apart.** Raw logs are kept unedited, including the pre-step samples, every run and every failed run; every value computed by a model, a fit or a simulation (the identified K and τ, the predicted metrics, the lag-model and sampled-model predictions, a redesigned gain) is labelled as computed; synthetic data are labelled synthetic in every table, figure and caption, and the title says SYNTHETIC. Presenting a simulation or a synthetic dataset as a measurement fails row 5 outright.

Weights are proposed and will be calibrated in pilots. **Minimum thresholds apply in the modelling, experiment and safety rows: presentation quality never compensates for a wrong model, an undated prediction, a missing uncertainty or an unsafe method.**

| Dimension | Weight | 4 (exemplary) | 3 (proficient) | 2 (developing) | 1 (insufficient) | Minimum |

## Grading Part D of the exam

Questions 23 and 24 are graded on the same rows, scaled to a question: row 2 (in 23, the tail-line identification with standard errors, the dated prediction with its uncertainty and a correct E_n; in 24, every wrong number recomputed with the right definition: the margin from L, the alias by folding, the disturbance error from the injection point), row 4 (in 23, ranked causes using the rate dependence, the lag and sampled models with E_n, and a discriminating follow-up with predicted outcomes; in 24, every error found and the correct item left alone), and row 5 (honest conclusions, measured versus predicted versus computed versus synthetic kept apart, and the hard requirements and safety limits named). A learner who answers question 23 with their own `p09-servo-control` data attaches the dated prediction file, the safety-test log and the completed safety box; without them the answer is graded as if the synthetic tables had been used, and the own-data claim is not recorded.

## Passing Phase 09

Pass requires: exam Parts A–D each at proficient level or above for the competencies they tag, **and** the mini-project at or above the minimum in every row with a weighted score of 3.0 or more. Report the outcome per competency as *practice completed*, *self-assessed mastery*, or *externally reviewed mastery* (the last only when a peer, mentor, or club reviewer has graded the report against this rubric). A mini-project completed with synthetic data only is recorded as *practice completed* for competency 4 until a measured report is graded. None of these is a credential, and nothing in this phase qualifies anyone to design, tune or approve the flight-control or actuator software of a real aircraft or spacecraft.
