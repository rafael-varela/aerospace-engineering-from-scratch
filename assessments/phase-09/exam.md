# Phase 09 Exam: Signals, Systems & Control

Integrated assessment of the whole phase. Suggested self-assessment mode: closed reference, a time limit chosen and recorded in advance (the exam has not been piloted, so no duration is suggested), no code except in Part C, no computer algebra in Part B; a scientific calculator (or Python used only as a calculator) is allowed for the arithmetic in Part B, since several questions need the roots of a cubic or quartic, a complex number's magnitude and angle, a 2 × 2 or 4 × 4 Routh array, an exponential and a logarithm. Treat `numpy.roots` and `numpy.linalg.eigvals` as calculator keys; anything that designs, simulates or plots for you is code. There is no enforced lock; the point is to find out what you can do unaided. Solutions and pass thresholds are in `solutions/phase-09/exam/`.

Report your result per competency: (1) mathematical and physical reasoning, (2) analytical problem solving, (3) computational competence, (4) engineering interpretation. Questions are tagged with the competencies they assess. Every question is written over an aerospace control loop: a pitch-attitude autopilot, a control-surface servo actuator, an altitude hold, an IMU's anti-aliasing and digital filtering, and a reaction-wheel satellite. The control theory is what is being tested; the framing is there so that you also practise deciding which model applies and what it assumes. The organising idea of the phase is *model, analyse, design, verify*: in every question write the model and its assumptions first, analyse it in the time or frequency domain, design what is asked, and check the main result by an independent route (a second method, a limiting case, a simulation of the unreduced model, or the oracle the lesson used). SI units throughout, angles in degrees unless a formula needs radians, frequencies in rad/s unless given in Hz (ω = 2πf). Conventions unless a question says otherwise: negative unity feedback; the loop transfer function L(s) is the product of every block around the loop, broken at the error; phase margin PM = 180° + ∠L(jω_c) at the gain crossover |L(jω_c)| = 1, gain margin GM = 1/|L(jω₁₈₀)| at a phase crossover, delay margin PM/ω_c with PM in radians; S = 1/(1 + L), T = L/(1 + L), Ms = max|S|, Mt = max|T|; overshoot in percent of the final value, rise time 10–90 %, settling time the last entry into a ±2 % band; dB = 20 log₁₀ of a magnitude ratio; discrete poles map as z = e^{sT}.

The models in this exam are representative numbers chosen for teaching, not any specific aircraft's, actuator's or spacecraft's data; each question states its own. Every data set in this exam is invented for the exam and is labelled synthetic; nothing here is a measurement, no table was produced by a kit you have built, and no simulation result in a question or in the key is a measurement. Part D question 23 is the exception in the other direction: if you have done the `p09-servo-control` experiment, answer it with *your own* data and say so; the synthetic tables are for learners without the kit.
