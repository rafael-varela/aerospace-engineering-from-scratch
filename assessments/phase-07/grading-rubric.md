# Phase 07 Grading rubric

Used for the Phase 07 mini-project and for grading the exam's Part D. **The mini-project is the cooling-curve report from P07.15** (Home Lab: Cooling Curve, `projects/p07-cooling-curve`): the temperature of a small aluminium bar, warmed in tap-hot water no hotter than 60 °C and hung in still room air, recorded as it cools and compared with a prediction written down and dated before the bar went into the water. It is the only physical experiment of the phase, and it exercises the whole of what the phase is about, which is conservation plus the second law with every result checked by an independent route: the learner writes the first law for the bar as a closed system (lesson 02), predicts the convective coefficient from the natural-convection correlations at the film temperature with Lienhard Table A.6 properties (lesson 13) and the radiative coefficient from the exact linearisation of gray-body exchange with an emittance from Lienhard Table 10.1 (lesson 14), checks the Biot number and compares the lumped model with a distributed series or finite-volume solution (lesson 12), predicts τ = ρcV/(hA) with an uncertainty budget (`P00.14`) in which the convection correlation and the emittance dominate, measures with the probe-lag transient identified and removed, fits a weighted log-linear line and a nonlinear exponential with the room temperature fixed at its measured value, reads χ² to decide whether one exponential is enough, compares by normalised error, and must explain a discrepancy that is usually a temperature-dependent h, a draught, an emittance or a wrongly chosen T∞, rather than a fault in the energy balance. Windowed time constants test the θ^(1/4) law of natural convection independently of the correlation's absolute level, and a second, black-taped bar separates convection from radiation, so a good report contains a real model test, not only a fitted τ. The computational lessons (01–14) are graded through their own tests and the exam, not here, because they involve no measurement; lessons 10 and 11 are computational only and never have a physical part. A learner without a kit completes the mini-project with the SYNTHETIC dataset in `projects/p07-cooling-curve/data/synthetic/` (seed 715), labels it SYNTHETIC in every file name, table, caption and plot title where it appears, and is graded on the same rows except that the experiment-design row grades the *design* (prediction, procedure, uncertainty budget) and the report must say that no physical measurement was made; such a report is recorded as *practice completed*, never as a measured result.

**Hard requirements for the cooling-curve report** (and for the exam's Part D question 20 when answered with the learner's own data):

1. **Safety box completed.** The report contains the project's safety box (`projects/p07-cooling-curve/safety.md`, in the format of `projects/p05-cantilever/safety.md`: hazards, energy limits, controls, supervision, location, regulations, stop criteria, disposal, not permitted), filled in for the setup actually used, with any deviation from the procedure stated. **A report without a completed safety box is not graded**, whatever else it contains. The box must record: tap-hot water no hotter than 60 °C, checked with the thermometer before the bar went in, every time; no boiling water, kettle at the boil, stove, oven, flame, heat gun, hot plate, microwave heating of metal or soldering iron; the bar handled with tongs while warm; battery or USB power only for any electronics; no glass containers; water kept away from the microcontroller and the computer; and the stop criteria (bar too hot to touch through a folded cloth, water above 60 °C, any electronics wet).
2. **Prediction dated before measurement.** `data/prediction.md` holds the measured dimensions and mass of the bar with uncertainties, the room temperature, V, A, L_c and ρcV, h_conv, h_rad and h at the chosen excess, the Biot number, the predicted τ with its budget term by term, and the predicted τ in each third of the run, and the date and time; that date precedes the first raw-data file. A prediction written or revised after the data were seen is reported as a post-hoc fit and graded as no prediction (row 3 at most *developing*); a later correction is appended with its own date, never edited in.
3. **Uncertainty reported.** Every measured and every predicted number carries a standard uncertainty with its type (A, B or combined) and the coverage factor if expanded; every comparison is a normalised error, not "close". A χ²/dof far from 1 is reported and interpreted, not hidden by Birge scaling alone. A number without an uncertainty is graded *insufficient* in row 2.
4. **Measurement, computation and synthetic data kept apart.** Raw readings are kept unedited, including the probe-lag transient, every room reading and the `note` column; every value computed by a model, a fit or a simulation (the predicted τ, the fitted τ and h, the series solution, the variable-h factor) is labelled as computed; SYNTHETIC data are labelled SYNTHETIC in every table, figure and caption. Presenting a calculation or a SYNTHETIC dataset as a measurement fails row 5 outright.

Weights are proposed and will be calibrated in pilots. **Minimum thresholds apply in the thermodynamics, experiment and safety rows: presentation quality never compensates for a wrong energy balance, an undated prediction, a missing uncertainty or an unsafe method.**

## Grading Part D of the exam

Questions 20 and 21 are graded on the same rows, scaled to a question: row 2 (the fits with χ² and a correct E_n, h with its uncertainty; in 21, every wrong number recomputed with absolute temperatures, the sunlit case and the correct correlation), row 4 (ranked causes using the windowed τ and their ratio, the free-T∞ trap, a discriminating follow-up with its predicted outcome; in 21, the out-of-range correlation and the missing environment case found, the recuperator rejected with its reason, and the correct item left alone), and row 5 (honest conclusions, measured versus predicted versus SYNTHETIC kept apart, and the hard requirements and safety limits named, including the computational-only rule of lessons 10 and 11). A learner who answers question 20 with their own `p07-cooling-curve` data attaches the dated prediction file and the completed safety box; without them the answer is graded as if the SYNTHETIC record had been used, and the own-data claim is not recorded.

## Passing Phase 07

Pass requires: exam Parts A–D each at proficient level or above for the competencies they tag, **and** the mini-project at or above the minimum in every row with a weighted score of 3.0 or more. Report the outcome per competency as *practice completed*, *self-assessed mastery*, or *externally reviewed mastery* (the last only when a peer, mentor, or club reviewer has graded the report against this rubric). A mini-project completed with the SYNTHETIC dataset only is recorded as *practice completed* for competency 4 until a measured report is graded. None of these is a credential, and nothing in this phase qualifies anyone to design, analyse or approve real engines, pressure vessels, combustors or spacecraft thermal systems.
