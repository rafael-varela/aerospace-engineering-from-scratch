Integrated assessment of the whole phase. Suggested self-assessment mode: closed reference, no code except in Part C, no computer algebra in Part B; a scientific calculator (or Python used only as a calculator) is allowed for the arithmetic in Part B, since several questions need fractional powers, logarithms and a root found by trial. **There is no time limit.** Take as long as you need, in as many sittings as you like, and record whether you worked closed-reference throughout; the exam has not been piloted, so no duration is suggested or implied. There is no enforced lock; the point is to find out what you can do unaided. Solutions and pass thresholds are in `solutions/phase-07/exam/`.

Report your result per competency: (1) mathematical and physical reasoning, (2) analytical problem solving, (3) computational competence, (4) engineering interpretation. Questions are tagged with the competencies they assess. Every question is written over an aerospace system; the thermodynamics is what is being tested, and the framing is there so that you also practise deciding which model applies and what it assumes. The organising idea of the phase is *conservation plus the second law*: in every question draw the system or control-volume boundary first, write the mass and energy balance, say what generates entropy, say what the model assumes and where it fails, and check the main result by an independent route (a closed form, a limit, a second method, a conservation law closing, or a measurement). SI units throughout; temperatures in kelvin in every formula.

Constants and conventions (the phase's own, used in every lesson): R_U = 8.31446261815324 J/(mol·K) (CODATA 2018); σ = 5.670374419 × 10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴); g₀ = 9.80665 m/s²; p° = 1 bar for the NASA data and 1 atm = 101 325 Pa; the dead state and sea-level ambient are T₀ = 288.15 K and p₀ = 101 325 Pa unless a question says otherwise; the solar irradiation at 1 AU is G_s = 1361 W/m². ## Part A: Concepts

**1.** (1, 4) Systems, state and the first law. (a) For each of these, say whether you would analyse it as a closed system or a control volume, draw the boundary, and say what crosses it: a COPV pressurant tank during blowdown; the same tank with its valve shut, warming on the pad; a combustor in steady operation; a sealed ammonia tank soaking in the sun. Say which of them is steady and which is not, and why the blowdown tank's balance needs enthalpy for the leaving gas but internal energy for the gas inside. (b) State the state postulate for a simple compressible substance. Say why (T, h) does not fix the state of an ideal gas, why (T, p) does not fix the state of a saturated mixture, and name a pair that does in each case. (c) Derive cp − cv = R for an ideal gas from h = u + pv. Using equipartition, give the values of cp/R for a monatomic gas and for a diatomic gas with vibration frozen and fully active, and explain why cp of air rises from 1004.7 J/(kg·K) at 298 K to 1181.7 J/(kg·K) at 1250 K. (d) The NASA polynomials make h of dry air −5.434 kJ/kg at 298.15 K. Explain where the negative value comes from, why it never matters for compression or heating of air, and why it is exactly what makes reacting-mixture energy balances work without separate "heat of reaction" bookkeeping. Say which lesson-02 assumption makes the blowdown energy balance close to round-off whatever the integrator's step size.

**2.** (1) The second law and exergy. (a) State the Kelvin–Planck and Clausius statements; derive the Carnot bound η ≤ 1 − T_C/T_H from the Clausius inequality ∮δQ/T ≤ 0 for a two-reservoir cycle; and say why S_gen = −Σ Q_k/T_k ≥ 0 is a test any claimed cycle must pass. (b) Write the Gibbs relations, say why they may be used to compute Δs for an irreversible process, and derive s_gen = R ln(p₁/p₂) for throttling an ideal gas. Give the small-ΔT behaviour of S_gen for heat Q crossing a fixed ΔT and for two equal bodies equilibrating, and explain why the exponents differ. (c) Define the dead state and the flow exergy ψ = (h − h₀) − T₀(s − s₀) + V²/2. Show that ψ is positive at p₀ for any T ≠ T₀ and equals RT₀ ln(p/p₀) at T₀, so that it is negative below p₀; give a physical reading of the negative value. Derive the Gouy–Stodola theorem (exergy destroyed = T₀S_gen) by combining the steady energy and entropy balances of a control volume. (d) Explain why a combustor, which loses no energy, is the largest exergy destroyer in a gas turbine, and prove that a compressor's second-law efficiency exceeds its isentropic efficiency.

**3.** (1, 4) Mixtures, reactions and equilibrium. (a) State Dalton's and Amagat's rules, show that for ideal gases they give the same mole fractions, and say why they differ for real gases at high pressure. Derive the entropy of mixing −R Σ x_i ln x_i and explain why it is entropy *generated*. Say why engineers count water per kilogram of dry air and why recirculating cabin air cannot change the cabin's humidity at steady state. (b) Define the equivalence ratio, the lower and higher heating values, and the adiabatic flame temperature. Explain why the complete-combustion T_ad does not depend on pressure and why it peaks at stoichiometric. (c) *Computational only.* Show that equilibrium at fixed T and p is a minimum of G subject to element conservation; write the Lagrange conditions μ_j/RT = Σ_i a_ij π_i and say what the element potentials π_i mean; derive the law of mass action Π(x_j p/p°)^ν_j = exp(−ΔG°/RT) from them. Use Le Chatelier's principle, with the sign of ΔH° and of the change in moles, to say how temperature and pressure move the dissociation of water. (d) Explain why equilibrium lowers a stoichiometric flame by more than 100 K but a lean turbine-inlet flame by only a few kelvin, and name one place in an engine where the equilibrium assumption itself fails and which way the error goes.

**4.** (1) Heat transfer. (a) State Fourier's law, give the units of k, α = k/(ρc) and the sheet conductance kt, and say why layers in series add L/k while layers side by side add kt. Explain why a vertex-centred finite-volume model of conduction closes its global energy balance to round-off rather than to truncation error, and why the explicit scheme needs αΔt/Δx² ≤ ½ in 1-D while the implicit one does not. (b) Define the Biot number and the penetration depth √(αt), and say what each tells you about whether a transient wall may be treated as a lump or as a semi-infinite solid. (c) Define Re, Pr, Nu, Gr, Ra and St and say which is a property of the fluid alone. Derive Nu_L = 2 Nu_x(L) for the laminar flat plate and the laminar Reynolds–Colburn analogy St Pr^(2/3) = c_f/2. Say why a correlation must not be used outside its stated range, with one example from lesson 13. (d) Define the view factor as a double area integral and prove reciprocity and the summation rule from it. Write the two radiosity equations (known T, known net Q), say why a reradiating wall's emittance does not affect the exchange between the other surfaces, and explain with Kirchhoff's law and the Planck spectrum how a white paint can absorb little sunlight and still emit well in the infrared.
