# Phase 08 Grading rubric

This rubric grades the Phase 08 mini-project and the exam's Part D. **The mini-project is the flow-and-pressure report from P08.19** (Home Lab: Flow & Pressure Measurement, `projects/p08-flow-pressure`). It has two experiments. In the first, a bottle drains through a small hole: the learner fits a discharge coefficient to the level against time and compares it with Torricelli's law. In the second, a water U-tube manometer reads the dynamic pressure from a straw pitot probe in the stream of a guarded fan or a hair dryer on cool, and gives an airspeed. Both are compared with predictions written down and dated before the first reading. It is the only physical experiment of the phase, and it exercises the phase's organising idea: *conservation of mass, momentum and energy, applied to a control volume or to a fluid particle, with a model for the stress*, with every result checked by an independent route.

The learner works through the following steps:
- applies mass conservation to the bottle as a control volume, and Bernoulli (energy along a streamline, inviscid stress) from the free surface to the jet (lessons 03 and 04);
- uses hydrostatics for the manometer (lesson 01) and Bernoulli at the probe's stagnation point for the airspeed (lesson 04);
- uses dimensional analysis to show which groups the draining time depends on (lesson 05);
- computes the Reynolds numbers of the jet, the probe and the fan to decide whether viscosity matters (lessons 05, 06 and 16);
- predicts with an uncertainty budget (`P00.14`) in which the hole diameter enters squared;
- measures the level against time and the manometer deflection with their resolution limits;
- fits √h against t (linear in t if Torricelli holds) with both Type A and Type B terms, and compares by normalised error.

The learner must also explain any discrepancy. In this lab it is usually a datum error (a ruler zeroed at the bottle's base rather than at the hole), a hole that is not sharp-edged, or a manometer reading at its resolution limit. It is rarely a fault in the conservation laws. The √h-against-t test checks Torricelli's law independently of C_d, because the law predicts a straight line whatever C_d is. A free-offset fit, or a measured hole height, then separates a datum error from a change of C_d. A second airspeed route (an anemometer at the enhanced tier, or the fan's rated flow, labelled as a manufacturer's figure) gives an independent check on the manometer, so a good report contains a real model test, not only a fitted coefficient.

Two lessons are graded elsewhere. Lesson 18's lid-driven-cavity solver is graded through its own tests and the exam's question 24, not here, because it involves no measurement. The turbulence lessons' data analyses (13–15) are graded through their tests and questions 17–19.

A learner without a kit completes the mini-project with the synthetic dataset in `projects/p08-flow-pressure/data/synthetic/` and labels it synthetic everywhere it appears. The report is graded on the same rows, with two differences: the experiment-design row grades the *design* (prediction, procedure, uncertainty budget), and the report must say that no physical measurement was made. Such a report is recorded as *practice completed*, never as a measured result.

**Hard requirements for the flow-and-pressure report** (and for the exam's Part D question 23 when answered with the learner's own data):

1. **Safety box completed.** The report contains the project's safety box (`projects/p08-flow-pressure/safety.md`, in the format of `projects/p05-cantilever/safety.md`: hazards, energy limits, controls, supervision, location, regulations, stop criteria, disposal, not permitted). It is filled in for the setup actually used, with any deviation from the procedure stated. **A report without a completed safety box is not graded**, whatever else it contains. The box must record:
   - water at room temperature, no more than 2 L in total;
   - the fan guarded (finger-proof grille) and drawing at most 30 W;
   - the hair dryer used on its cool setting only and kept away from water (separate tables, the manometer's water sealed or below the dryer, never in the same basin);
   - no compressed gas, no pressurised containers (no squeezing or pressurising bottles to drive flow);
   - no mains appliance near spilled water, and spills mopped before switching anything on;
   - the stop criteria.
2. **Prediction dated before measurement.** `data/prediction.md` (or the equivalent) holds the predicted draining time (or the level against time) from the measured d, D and h₀ and a stated C_d, and the predicted manometer reading or airspeed for each air source, each with its uncertainty, and the date. That date precedes the first raw-data file. A prediction written or revised after the data were seen is reported as a post-hoc fit and graded as no prediction (row 3 at most *developing*).
3. **Uncertainty reported.** Every measured and every predicted number carries a standard uncertainty with its type (A, B or combined) and the coverage factor if expanded. A manometer reading carries its resolution term even when repeated readings agree. Every comparison is a normalised error, not "close". A number without an uncertainty is graded *insufficient* in row 2.
4. **Measurement, computation and synthetic data kept apart.** Raw readings are kept unedited, including every video frame time and every manometer reading. Every value computed by a model, a fit or a simulation (the predicted draining time, the fitted C_d, an airspeed, a Reynolds number) is labelled as computed. A manufacturer's rating is labelled as such. Synthetic data are labelled synthetic in every table, figure and caption. Presenting a calculation, a rating or a synthetic dataset as a measurement fails row 5 outright.

Weights are proposed and will be calibrated in pilots. **Minimum thresholds apply in the physics, experiment and safety rows. Presentation quality never compensates for wrong physics, an undated prediction, a missing uncertainty or an unsafe method.**

## Grading Part D of the exam

Questions 23 and 24 are graded on the same rows, scaled to a question:
- **Row 2:** in 23, the fits with both uncertainty types, the resolution and angle terms, and a correct E_n; in 24, the observed orders, the Richardson values and the GCI computed correctly.
- **Row 4:** in 23, the datum error diagnosed from the residual pattern and the description, and the free-offset fit compared by χ²; in 24, each of the four statements judged correctly, with the automatic divergence and the "benchmark is not exact" points made, and verification kept apart from validation.
- **Row 5:** honest conclusions; measured, predicted, computed, published and synthetic values kept apart; and the hard requirements and safety limits named.

A learner who answers question 23 with their own `p08-flow-pressure` data attaches the dated prediction file and the completed safety box. Without them the answer is graded as if the synthetic tables had been used, and the own-data claim is not recorded.

## Passing Phase 08

To pass, all of the following must hold:
- exam Parts A–D each at proficient level or above for the competencies they tag;
- the mini-project at or above the minimum in every row;
- a weighted score of 3.0 or more on the mini-project.

Report the outcome per competency as *practice completed*, *self-assessed mastery*, or *externally reviewed mastery* (the last only when a peer, mentor, or club reviewer has graded the report against this rubric). A mini-project completed with synthetic data only is recorded as *practice completed* for competency 4 until a measured report is graded. None of these is a credential. Nothing in this phase qualifies anyone to calibrate or sign off a real aircraft's air-data system, size a real propellant feed system, or certify a CFD code for design use.
