# Phase 10 Grading rubric

Used for the Phase 10 mini-project, for grading the exam's Part D, and, through the per-lesson table below, for deciding when each lesson's work counts as proficient. **The mini-project is the bench-rig report from P10.23** (Home Lab: Airfoil Lift & Drag on a Bench Rig, `projects/p10-airfoil-bench`). It covers the lift and drag of a foam or card section in the jet of a guarded fan with a straw-honeycomb straightener, measured against angle of attack on a kitchen-scale lift balance and a pivot-arm drag balance, and compared with a prediction written down and dated before the first reading.

It is the only physical experiment of the phase. It exercises the whole of what the phase is about, which is *a hierarchy of models, each checked by an independent route*:

- The learner reduces balance readings to coefficients with the axis and moment conventions of lesson 01, and an uncertainty budget (`P00.14`) in which the tiny dynamic pressure (q ≈ 10 Pa at 4 m/s) dominates.
- They predict cl from thin-airfoil theory (lesson 04) and their own panel code (lesson 05), with and without a boundary-layer estimate (lessons 06 and 14).
- They turn the section prediction into a finite-span one for the end-plated model (lessons 09 and 10).
- They estimate the drag with the friction and form-factor reasoning of lesson 13.
- They compare all of it with Ladson's authentic NACA 0012 data at a Reynolds number roughly seventy times higher.
- They must explain the gap physically. At the bench's Re ≈ 3 × 10⁴ the laminar layer separates (lesson 14), so the lift-curve slope is lower, cd is several times higher, and the stall is earlier and softer (lesson 15). The explanation must not be "experimental error".

Two features make a good report contain a real model test, not only a fitted curve:

- A second airspeed method (a vane anemometer beside the pitot-and-manometer, or the reverse) separates an error in q, which scales CL and CD by the same factor and leaves L/D unchanged, from a change in the aerodynamics.
- An inverted-model run separates jet angularity from a model asymmetry.

Lessons 21 and 22 (tool validation, and the V&V of a vortex lattice against authentic finite-wing data) are graded through their own tests, the per-lesson table below and the exam's question 23, not here, because they involve no physical measurement.

A learner without a kit completes the mini-project with the seeded synthetic dataset of `projects/p10-airfoil-bench/data/synthetic/`, labels it synthetic everywhere it appears, and is graded on the same rows. The experiment-design row then grades the *design* (prediction, procedure, uncertainty budget), and the report must say that no physical measurement was made. Such a report is recorded as *practice completed*, never as a measured result.

**Hard requirements for the bench-rig report** (and for the exam's Part D question 25 when it is answered with the learner's own data):

1. **Safety box completed.** The report contains the project's safety box (`projects/p10-airfoil-bench/safety.md`, in the format of `projects/p05-cantilever/safety.md`: hazards, energy limits, controls, supervision, location, regulations, stop criteria, disposal, not permitted), filled in for the setup actually used, with any deviation from the procedure stated. **A report without a completed safety box is not graded**, whatever else it contains. The box must record:
   - the fan rated at most 50 W, read from its label;
   - the fan guarded on both sides, front and back grilles intact, undamaged and finger-proof, checked before every session;
   - the fan on a stable, level surface where it cannot tip or walk;
   - no propellers, drone or model-aircraft motors or unguarded blades, ever;
   - the fan unplugged (not only switched off) before the model, the angle, the balance or the straightener is adjusted, and before anyone reaches near the fan;
   - no cutting tools near the running fan;
   - hot-wire foam cutting only with adult supervision and ventilation, otherwise a craft knife with the fan unplugged;
   - the cable routed so it cannot be tripped over or pulled into the fan;
   - loose paper, hair, scarves, string and straws kept away from the intake;
   - the model, end plates, sting and balance secured so nothing can be blown into the fan, with nobody standing between the fan and the model;
   - safety glasses whenever the fan runs;
   - the stop criteria respected: the fan rattles or walks, a grille is damaged or anything touches one, or the motor smells or feels hot.
2. **Prediction dated before measurement.** `data/prediction.md` (or the equivalent) holds the predicted airspeed and q with their uncertainty, the Reynolds number, and the predicted CL and CD at each planned angle with their uncertainty bands (thin-airfoil and panel cl, the finite-span band for the end-plated model, and a low-Re drag estimate), with the date. That date precedes the first raw-data file. A prediction written or revised after the data were seen is reported as a post-hoc fit and graded as no prediction (row 3 at most *developing*).
3. **Uncertainty reported.** Every measured and every predicted number carries a standard uncertainty with its type (A, B or combined) and the coverage factor if expanded. The budget includes the scale resolutions, the arm ratio from its calibration, the tares, the airspeed method, the model dimensions, and the blockage band (carried as an uncertainty on q, since in an open jet its sign is uncertain). Every comparison is a normalised error, not "close". A number without an uncertainty is graded *insufficient* in row 2.
4. **Measurement, computation and synthetic data kept apart.** Raw scale and manometer readings are kept unedited, including every fan-off reading, every tare and every repeated run. Every value computed by a model, a fit or a reduction (the predicted CL, the panel cl, the fitted slope, the reduced CL and CD) is labelled as computed. Ladson's values are labelled authentic with their report and table. Synthetic data are labelled synthetic in every table, figure and caption. Presenting a calculation or a synthetic dataset as a measurement fails row 5 outright.

Weights are proposed and will be calibrated in pilots. **Minimum thresholds apply in the aerodynamics, experiment and safety rows: presentation quality never compensates for wrong aerodynamics, an undated prediction, a missing uncertainty or an unsafe method.**

| Dimension | Weight | 4 (exemplary) | 3 (proficient) | 2 (developing) | 1 (insufficient) | Minimum |

In every row, "tests pass" means the lesson's public tests pass against the learner's own `starter/main.py`; for the computational lessons, the learner's implementation must also pass the seeded cases planned in `parameterized-cases/` once they exist. ## Grading Part D of the exam

Questions 25 and 26 are graded on the same rows, scaled to a question:

- **Row 2:** in 25, the reduction with both uncertainty types, the blockage band and a correct E_n; in 26, every number recomputed with the right model.
- **Row 4:** in 25, the airspeed tested by a second method, L/D used as a q-free check, the inverted runs used, the causes ranked with what the data rule in or out, a discriminating follow-up with predicted outcomes, and the Re gap against Ladson's data explained; in 26, the out-of-bounds inputs and the 2-D-to-3-D confusions found, and the correct item left alone.
- **Row 5:** honest conclusions, measured, computed, authentic and synthetic values kept apart, and the hard requirements and safety limits named.

A learner who answers question 25 with their own `p10-airfoil-bench` data attaches the dated prediction file and the completed safety box. Without them the answer is graded as if the synthetic tables had been used, and the own-data claim is not recorded.

## Passing Phase 10

Pass requires all three of these:

- exam Parts A–D each at proficient level or above for the competencies they tag;
- every lesson's row in the per-lesson table met;
- the mini-project at or above the minimum in every row, with a weighted score of 3.0 or more.

Report the outcome per competency as *practice completed*, *self-assessed mastery*, or *externally reviewed mastery*. The last applies only when a peer, mentor, or club reviewer has graded the report against this rubric. A mini-project completed with synthetic data only is recorded as *practice completed* for competency 4 until a measured report is graded. None of these is a credential, and nothing in this phase qualifies anyone to certify an aircraft's aerodynamic data, its performance or its handling.
