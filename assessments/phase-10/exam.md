# Phase 10 Exam: Aerodynamics

Integrated assessment of the whole phase. Suggested self-assessment mode: closed reference, a time limit chosen and recorded in advance (the exam has not been piloted, so no duration is suggested), no code except in Part C, no computer algebra in Part B. A scientific calculator (or Python used only as a calculator) is allowed for the arithmetic in Part B, because several questions need complex arithmetic (question 21), a 2 × 2 solve (question 14), a short least-squares fit (question 23), inverse trigonometric functions and a few logarithms. There is no enforced lock; the point is to find out what you can do unaided. Solutions and pass thresholds are in `solutions/phase-10/exam/`.

Report your result per competency: (1) mathematical and physical reasoning, (2) analytical problem solving, (3) computational competence, (4) engineering interpretation. Questions are tagged with the competencies they assess. Every question is set on an aerospace vehicle; the aerodynamics is what is being tested, and the framing is there so that you also practise deciding which model applies and what it assumes. The organising idea of the phase is *a hierarchy of models, each checked by an independent route*: thin-airfoil theory, panel methods and boundary layers in 2-D; horseshoe vortices, lifting line and vortex lattices in 3-D; momentum, blade element and BEM for rotors; quasi-steady, Theodorsen and Wagner in time. In every question say where the model you use sits in that hierarchy, what it neglects and where it fails, and check the main result by an independent route (a second model, a limiting case, a closed form, or the observed order of a convergence study). SI units throughout; knots and feet per minute appear only where helicopter practice uses them.

Conventions (as in every Phase 10 lesson): α positive nose-up; lift perpendicular and drag parallel to the free stream; pitching moment positive nose-up, about the quarter chord unless stated; x along the chord from the leading edge, z up; 2-D section coefficients lower-case (cl, cd, cm), 3-D wing and aircraft coefficients upper-case (CL, CD, Cm). Circulation Γ is positive clockwise for a lifting airfoil in a left-to-right stream, so that L′ = ρV∞Γ is positive. Air is ISA sea level unless a question says otherwise: ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, μ = 1.789 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s, ν = 1.461 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s, a = 340.3 m/s, g = 9.81 m/s². Room air for the bench rig: ρ = 1.204 kg/m³, μ = 1.81 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s, water density 998 kg/m³.

## Part A: Concepts

**1.** (1, 4) Coefficients, similarity and the hierarchy of models. (a) Define the dynamic pressure q, cl, cd and cm (with their reference area and length), the Reynolds number and the Mach number. Use dimensional analysis to say why, for a body of fixed shape at a fixed attitude, every force coefficient depends only on Re and M. (b) Name the models of the phase's hierarchy in each of its four families (2-D sections, 3-D wings, rotors, time-dependent flow). For each model say what it neglects, and give the independent check the phase uses to verify it. (c) Define code verification, solution verification and validation, give one example of each from the phase, and say why "the code agrees with the wind tunnel" is not, on its own, a validation statement. (d) Which results of a low-speed tunnel test of a section transfer to the full-scale wing with small corrections, and which do not? Answer for the lift-curve slope, the zero-lift angle, the aerodynamic centre, the minimum drag and cl_max, with the physical reason. What does a Reynolds-number gap of a factor of about 170, between the bench rig of lesson 23 and the trainer in cruise, mean for each of them?

**2.** (1) Circulation, the Kutta condition and induced drag. (a) Define the circulation Γ and state the phase's sign convention. State the Kutta–Joukowski theorem. Explain why steady potential flow past an airfoil has a one-parameter family of solutions, what the Kutta condition requires at a sharp trailing edge, and how Kelvin's theorem and the starting vortex explain the circulation a real airfoil acquires. (b) State Helmholtz's vortex theorems and show how they lead to the horseshoe vortex and the trailing vortex sheet. Explain why a finite wing has induced drag in inviscid flow when a 2-D airfoil has no drag at all (d'Alembert). (c) Explain why the downwash far downstream of a flat wake (the Trefftz plane) is twice the downwash at the lifting line, why the elliptic load gives the least induced drag for a given lift and span, and what you conclude when a code reports a span efficiency e = 1.03 for a planar wing. At fixed weight and speed, which single geometric parameter controls induced drag? (d) Thin-airfoil theory gives a lift slope of 2π per radian and puts the aerodynamic centre at the quarter chord whatever the camber. Say why, and say in which direction and by roughly how much 12 % thickness (inviscid) and the boundary layer (Ladson's NACA 0012 at Re = 2 × 10⁶) each move the real slope from 2π.

**3.** (1) Boundary layers, drag, Reynolds number and stall. (a) Define the displacement thickness δ*, the momentum thickness θ and the shape factor H, state the momentum-integral equation, and say what Thwaites' method, Michel's criterion, Head's method and the Squire–Young formula each contribute to a section-drag prediction. Say why one-way viscous-inviscid coupling over-predicts the lift slope. (b) Explain laminar separation, transition and the laminar separation bubble. Show that in Thwaites' method the laminar separation point does not depend on the Reynolds number, say what does depend on it, and say what decides whether a bubble closes on the airfoil. (c) Name the three stall types of NACA TN-2502. Say which one the NACA 0012 shows at Re = 2 × 10⁶ and why a Kirchhoff separation-point model fitted to those data becomes almost a step. Explain why an untwisted rectangular wing stalls first at its root, and what taper and washout each do to that. (d) Ladson's free-transition NACA 0012 zero-lift drag stays between 0.0062 and 0.0066 from Re = 2 × 10⁶ to 1.89 × 10⁷, while a fully turbulent skin-friction estimate falls by about 31 % over the same range. Explain why. Then say why cl_max must not be extrapolated from Ladson's range down to a small drone's Reynolds number.

**4.** (1, 4) Rotors, bodies, unsteady flow and ground effect. (a) For an actuator disk, derive from mass, momentum and energy conservation that half the final slipstream velocity increase occurs ahead of the disk. Say why no propeller can beat the Froude efficiency at its own thrust, and give the factor by which the ideal static power changes when the thrust doubles and when the diameter doubles. Define the figure of merit and say why it rises with disk loading for a given rotor. (b) Explain, from the apparent mass of its cross-sections, why a closed slender body in potential flow carries no lift but feels a nose-up couple 2qα·Vol, why that couple does not depend on the reference point, and what it does to an aircraft's neutral point. (c) Define the reduced frequency and the reduced time. Give the limits of Theodorsen's function C(k) as k → 0 and k → ∞ and of Wagner's function at s = 0 and s → ∞, with their physical meaning. State the relation between them, and say why the lift of a pitching section can *lead* the pitch at moderate k. (d) Explain with the method of images why a wing near the ground makes more lift at a given angle and less induced drag at a given lift. Say why the steady result is an upper bound on the cushioning during a real descending flare, and name one physical effect near the ground that a flat image lattice cannot represent.
