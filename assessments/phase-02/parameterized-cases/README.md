# Phase 02 parameterized validation cases

Unseen inputs for the computational problems, so answers cannot be copied. Each case is a JSON file `{"lesson": "P02.NN", "seed": int, "inputs": {...}, "expected": {...}, "tolerance": {...}}`. Generate new ones with `generate.py` (which uses the reference solutions) and check a learner's implementation with `run.py`. Expected values come from SciPy as the oracle (`solve_ivp` with rtol 10⁻¹⁰ for the ODE lessons, `scipy.linalg.expm`, `scipy.fft`, `scipy.stats` and `numpy.linalg.lstsq` for the rest) or from a closed form where one exists, and the tolerance is set so that a correct from-scratch implementation passes but a wrong sign in a characteristic root, a missing 1/N in a transform, a z quantile where a t quantile belongs, or an unnormalised posterior does not. Anything random inside a case (noise, Monte Carlo samples) is generated from the case's seed with `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)`, and statistical tolerances are stated with the number of samples that justifies them.

Cases shipped:

- `p02-01-rocket-burn-*.json`: m₀, m_dry, ṁ, v_e, g, and a drag law (none, linear or quadratic with a stated coefficient) → burn time, burnout velocity and altitude, and for the drag cases the terminal velocity and apex time (rtol 1e-6 against the closed form where one exists, rtol 1e-5 against `solve_ivp` otherwise; the RK4 result must converge at fourth order between two supplied step sizes).
- `p02-02-damped-oscillator-*.json`: m, c, k and initial conditions spanning the under-, critically and over-damped cases → ω_n, ζ, ω_d, the classification, the solution coefficients, and x(t) at listed times (rtol 1e-8; the classification must be right, and the critically damped case must not be reported as underdamped with ω_d = 0).
- `p02-05-state-transition-*.json`: random stable and unstable 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 matrices A, a time t, and an initial state → Φ(t), x(t), det Φ(t) and the eigenvalues (rtol 1e-8 against `expm`; det Φ must equal e^{t·tr A} to 1e-8 and Φ(t/2)² must equal Φ(t)).
- `p02-08-laplace-step-*.json`: coefficients of a second-order ODE with a step or impulse input and initial conditions → poles, DC gain, partial-fraction residues, and y(t) at listed times (rtol 1e-8 against the SymPy inverse transform; the residues must sum to the correct coefficient of the leading term and y(0) must match the initial condition).
- `p02-10-dft-*.json`: a sampled signal of N points (sum of tones plus seeded noise, possibly with an aliased tone) and f_s → the DFT magnitudes, the frequencies of the largest peaks, and the one-sided power spectrum (rtol 1e-10 against `scipy.fft.fft`; Parseval must hold to 1e-10; an aliased tone must be reported at its folded frequency).
- `p02-16-dispersion-*.json`: σ_x, σ_y, a correlation coefficient and a probability level → the ellipse scale k, its semi-axes and orientation, and the probability inside a given rectangle (rtol 1e-6 on the closed form; a Monte Carlo check with the case's seed and 10⁶ samples must agree to within 3 × 10⁻³, which is three standard errors for p ≈ 0.95).
- `p02-18-thrust-fit-*.json`: synthetic thrust-versus-pressure or thrust-versus-time samples with seeded Gaussian noise and a model order → the fitted parameters, s, R², the parameter standard errors, and the confidence and prediction half-widths at listed abscissae (rtol 1e-6 against `lstsq` and the analytic covariance; the residuals must be orthogonal to the design columns to 1e-10, and the half-widths must use the t quantile with n − p degrees of freedom, so a z quantile fails).
- `p02-20-failure-rate-*.json`: a Gamma prior, a failure count and an exposure, a requirement λ_req and a prediction horizon → the posterior parameters, mean, mode and 95% credible interval, P(λ > λ_req), and the predictive probability of zero failures (rtol 1e-8 against `scipy.stats.gamma`; a grid posterior must agree with the conjugate closed form to 1e-6 and must integrate to one).

These are written once the reference solutions for lessons 01, 02, 05, 08, 10, 16, 18 and 20 are merged; `generate.py` then produces them deterministically from a seed.
