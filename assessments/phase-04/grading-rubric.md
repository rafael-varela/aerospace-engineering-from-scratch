# Phase 04 Grading rubric

Used for the Phase 04 mini-project and for grading the exam's Part D. **The mini-project is the pendulum report from P04.06** (Oscillations & Waves, `projects/p04-pendulum`): the period of a string pendulum measured against length and against amplitude, compared with a prediction written down and dated before the first measurement. It is chosen over the drag-drop and electronics-bench reports because it exercises the whole of what the phase is about, which is conservation and modelling with every result checked against measurement: the learner derives the equation of motion from the torque about the pivot (lessons 01 and 04), checks the simulation by energy conservation (lesson 02), uses the exact elliptic-integral period and the θ₀²/16 series as the model whose limit is the small-angle law (lesson 06), predicts with an uncertainty budget (`P00.14`), measures with a frame-quantisation model and both Type A and Type B terms, compares with the normalised error, and must explain a discrepancy that is usually a modelling error (the length to the centre of mass, the bob's finite size, quadratic air damping) rather than a measurement one. A second length separates a length offset from a timing or g error, and the period ratio between two amplitudes tests the amplitude model independently of every multiplicative systematic, so a good report contains a real model test, not only a fit. The `p04-drag-drop` report from P04.01 (fall time with and without quadratic drag) and the `p04-electronics-bench` report from P04.15 (voltage, current and a sensor read by multimeter and microcontroller ADC) may be substituted by a learner who documents the same elements: a dated prediction with uncertainty, raw data, a comparison by normalised error, a ranked discrepancy investigation with one follow-up measurement, and the completed safety box; the rows below then apply to that experiment. A learner without a kit completes the mini-project with the synthetic dataset supplied in the lesson, labels it synthetic everywhere it appears, and is graded on the same rows except that the experiment-design row grades the *design* (prediction, procedure, uncertainty budget) and the report must say that no physical measurement was made; such a report is recorded as *practice completed*, never as a measured result.

**Hard requirements for every physical-experiment report in this phase** (mini-project and any substituted report, and the exam's Part D question 20 when answered with the learner's own data):

1. **Safety box completed.** The report contains the project's safety box (`projects/p04-<id>/safety.md`, in the format of `projects/p00-first-experiment/safety.md`: hazards, energy limits, controls, supervision, location, regulations, stop criteria, disposal, not permitted), filled in for the setup actually used, with any deviation from the procedure stated. **A report without a completed safety box is not graded**, whatever else it contains. For `p04-pendulum` the box must record string ≤ 2 m, bob ≤ 0.2 kg and a swing zone clear of people and screens; for `p04-drag-drop` the drop height, object mass and landing zone; for `p04-electronics-bench` battery or USB power only, never mains, supply ≤ 9 V DC, no lithium-cell charging circuits, every LED through a resistor, current ≤ 500 mA, no capacitors above 25 V or 1000 μF and capacitors discharged before touching, and soldering safety written down if the learner soldered.
2. **Prediction dated before measurement.** `data/prediction.md` (or the equivalent) holds the predicted values with their uncertainty and the date, and that date precedes the first raw-data file. A prediction written or revised after the data were seen is reported as a post-hoc fit and graded as no prediction (row 3 at most *developing*).
3. **Uncertainty reported.** Every measured and every predicted number carries a standard uncertainty with its type (A, B or combined) and the coverage factor if expanded; every comparison is a normalised error, not "close". A number without an uncertainty is graded *insufficient* in row 2.
4. **Measurement and simulation kept apart.** Raw data are kept unedited; every value computed by a model or simulation is labelled as computed; synthetic data are labelled synthetic in every table, figure and caption. Presenting a simulation or a synthetic dataset as a measurement fails row 5 outright.

Weights are proposed and will be calibrated in pilots. **Minimum thresholds apply in the physics, experiment and safety rows: presentation quality never compensates for wrong physics, an undated prediction, a missing uncertainty or an unsafe method.**

| Dimension | Weight | 4 (exemplary) | 3 (proficient) | 2 (developing) | 1 (insufficient) | Minimum |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| Correct physics, derivation, assumptions | 25% | Equation of motion derived from the torque about the pivot with the tension's zero moment stated; the conservation law (energy) used to check the model; small-angle law obtained as the limit of the elliptic-integral period, the θ₀²/16 term derived or justified by symmetry; effective length to the centre of mass defined; every assumption (point bob, massless inextensible string, rigid pivot, no damping) listed with a quantitative estimate of what it changes (finite bob (2/5)(r/L)², damping ζ²/2, amplitude θ₀²/16) and ranked | Model, limit and assumptions correct; the dominant neglected effects estimated | An assumption left implicit, or the amplitude correction quoted without its range of validity | Wrong period law, the small-angle law used at 45° without comment, or no statement of what the model neglects | 3 |
| Calculation and numerical correctness | 20% | Every reduction reproducible from the raw frame tables (or accelerometer logs) by the submitted code; the period simulation checked against `scipy.special.ellipk` and against energy conservation in tests; Type A and Type B terms computed and combined; T² against L fitted with the uncertainty of the slope and intercept; significant figures match the uncertainty | Correct results; code runs and regenerates the numbers; both uncertainty types present | Results plausible but the code does not reproduce them, or only one uncertainty type | Arithmetic, unit or convention errors (elliptic parameter m confused with modulus k, degrees in a radian formula); numbers without uncertainty | 2 |
| Experiment design, data quality, uncertainty | 20% | Prediction with an uncertainty budget recorded and dated before the first measurement; at least three lengths and four amplitudes (one ≤ 5° and one ≥ 30°) with at least five timings each over ten or more periods; the frame or sample quantisation modelled; the length measured to the centre of mass with its uncertainty; the budget shows which term limits the comparison and the design was chosen so that the amplitude effect is resolvable; all raw data kept | Dated prediction first; adequate repeats; both uncertainty types; raw data kept | Few repeats, one length only, or no Type B term, or raw data partly discarded | No prediction before measurement, or no uncertainty | 2 |
| Verification, validation, discrepancy investigation | 20% | Normalised errors at every point for both the small-angle and the amplitude-corrected model; the amplitude model tested independently of multiplicative systematics (period ratio between amplitudes); every E_n > 2 ranked by candidate cause with magnitudes, and one follow-up measurement (a second length, a re-measured centre of mass, a stopwatch-independent timing) done and interpreted with the outcome each hypothesis predicted; the simulation verified against its closed-form limit separately from validating the model against the data | Disagreements identified, ranked and plausibly explained; one follow-up proposed with its expected outcome | Disagreements noted but unexplained, or the model declared "confirmed" | "Experimental error", agreement claimed without a comparison, or a parameter (g, L) tuned until the data fit | 2 |
| Communication, reproducibility, safety documentation, honesty | 15% | Anyone could repeat the experiment and regenerate every number from the report; safety box complete and the stop criteria respected; figures captioned with units and k = 2 error bars; the report says what the evidence supports and what it does not (no model is "proven"), and keeps measured, computed and synthetic values visibly distinct | Clear report with code and data; safety box complete; limits stated | Report incomplete or hard to follow; limits implicit | Missing code or data, missing safety box, or a computed or synthetic value presented as a measurement | Safety box complete (hard requirement) |

## Grading Part D of the exam

Questions 20 and 21 are graded on the same rows, scaled to a question: row 2 (reduction with both uncertainty types and correct E_n), row 4 (ranked causes, a discriminating follow-up with predicted outcomes, an independent test of the model), and row 5 (honest conclusions, measured versus predicted versus synthetic kept apart, and the hard requirements named). A learner who answers question 20 with their own `p04-pendulum` or `p04-drag-drop` data attaches the dated prediction file and the completed safety box; without them the answer is graded as if the synthetic table had been used, and the own-data claim is not recorded.

## Competencies and mastery descriptors

| Competency | Practice completed | Self-assessed mastery | Externally reviewed mastery |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Mathematical and physical reasoning | Exam Part A attempted and graded against the key | Part A proficient: each answer names the conservation law or field equation, applies it, and states where the model fails | As self-assessed, confirmed by a reviewer who graded the answers against the key |
| 2 Analytical problem solving | Part B attempted | Eleven of fourteen Part B questions proficient with derivations shown | As self-assessed, confirmed by a reviewer |
| 3 Computational competence | Part C attempted and every lesson's tests run against the learner's own `starter/main.py` | Part C tests passing; the learner's own implementations pass the parameterized cases in `parameterized-cases/` for the computational lessons | As self-assessed, with the reviewer re-running the cases from a fresh seed |
| 4 Engineering interpretation | Part D attempted; mini-project report written (with synthetic data if no kit) | Part D proficient and the mini-project at or above every minimum with a weighted score of 3.0 | Mini-project graded against this rubric by a peer, mentor or club reviewer |

## Passing Phase 04

Pass requires: exam Parts A–D each at proficient level or above for the competencies they tag, **and** the mini-project at or above the minimum in every row with a weighted score of 3.0 or more. Report the outcome per competency as *practice completed*, *self-assessed mastery*, or *externally reviewed mastery* (the last only when a peer, mentor, or club reviewer has graded the report against this rubric). A mini-project completed with synthetic data only is recorded as *practice completed* for competency 4 until a measured report is graded. None of these is a credential.
