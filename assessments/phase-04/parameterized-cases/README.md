# Phase 04 parameterized validation cases

Unseen inputs for the computational problems, so answers cannot be copied. Each case is a JSON file `{"lesson": "P04.NN", "seed": int, "inputs": {...}, "expected": {...}, "tolerance": {...}}`. Generate new ones with `generate.py` (which uses the reference solutions) and check a learner's implementation with `run.py`. Expected values come from a closed form wherever the physics has one, and otherwise from SciPy as the oracle: `scipy.integrate.solve_ivp` at rtol 10⁻¹² with event location for trajectories, `scipy.special.ellipk` for the pendulum, `numpy.linalg.solve` for circuit networks, `scipy.integrate.quad` and `dblquad` for field and pattern integrals, and exact rational arithmetic for decay chains. Every case also contains a *limit* that the case's physics must reproduce exactly (zero drag, a circular orbit, a small amplitude, a single resistor, DC, the loop's own axis, the far field, a non-relativistic speed, zero elapsed time), so an implementation that returns the right number at the generated point by tuning cannot pass. Tolerances are set so that a correct from-scratch implementation passes and a specific, named physics or convention error fails; each bullet states both. Anything random inside a case (initial conditions, component values, noise added to a synthetic measurement, Monte Carlo samples) is generated from the case's seed with `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)`, and statistical tolerances are stated with the sample count that justifies them. No case contains a measurement; inputs that imitate a measurement are labelled `"synthetic": true` in the JSON, and `run.py` prints that label with the result.

Cases planned, one family per computational lesson:

- `p04-01-projectile-drag-*.json` (P04.01): mass, diameter and C_D of a light sphere (ping-pong to tennis ball, from the seed), launch speed 0–8 m/s and angle, release height 0.5–2.0 m, air density → time of flight, range, impact speed, and apex height with quadratic drag; plus the vertical-drop fall time. **Oracle:** for the vertical drop from rest, the closed form t = (v_t/g) arcosh(exp(gh/v_t²)); for the oblique case, `solve_ivp` (RK45 at rtol 10⁻¹², ground contact by a terminal event) on the coupled equations with drag along the total velocity. **Limit:** C_D = 0 must reproduce the vacuum parabola (range, apex, flight time) to 10⁻¹⁰. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁶ on every output, loose enough for any adaptive integrator at rtol ≤ 10⁻⁸ and for an event located by interpolation, tight enough that drag written as ½ρC_DA v_x² and v_y² separately (instead of ½ρC_DA|v|v), which for a ping-pong ball tossed horizontally at 3 m/s from 2 m shortens the flight time by 1.6% and lengthens the range by 2.3%, fails, as do a diameter used as a radius (area × 4) and a sign error that makes drag assist the fall.
- `p04-05-two-body-*.json` (P04.05): μ, a perigee radius and an eccentricity from the seed (0 ≤ e ≤ 0.9), a propagation time of 1–20 periods, a step count → state after propagation, specific energy and angular momentum histories from the learner's leapfrog and from their `solve_ivp` run. **Oracle:** Kepler's equation solved by `scipy.optimize.brentq` for the analytic position at the final time; vis-viva for the energy; h = √(μa(1 − e²)). **Limit:** e = 0 must give a circle with speed √(μ/r) and period 2π√(r³/μ) to 10⁻¹⁰. **Tolerance:** position to rtol 10⁻⁸ from the `solve_ivp` path; for leapfrog, the angular momentum must be conserved to 10⁻¹² relative (a central force gives it to round-off: at most 5 × 10⁻¹⁴ in the reference runs, so any non-symplectic or mis-ordered kick-drift-kick fails), the energy error must stay bounded with no secular trend (the change of |ΔE| over the run, from a linear fit, must be below 0.1 of its mean; a correct leapfrog gives at most 0.04 in reference runs of 20 orbits at 2000 and 4000 steps per orbit with e = 0, 0.5 and 0.9, while RK4 at the same steps gives 0.6 to 2, because its error accumulates), and for e ≥ 0.1 the maximum energy error must scale as h² between the two supplied step counts (observed order 2 ± 0.2; the reference runs give a ratio of 4.0 for e = 0.5 and 0.9, whereas for e = 0 the error is at round-off level and has no order, so the order check is skipped there); μ/r² used where μ/r³ × r is needed (a unit-vector slip) fails the circular limit at once.
- `p04-06-pendulum-*.json` (P04.06): length 0.2–2.0 m, g, amplitudes 1°–170° from the seed, a damping ratio (zero in half the cases) → period at each amplitude, the series T₀(1 + θ₀²/16 + 11θ₀⁴/3072), and, with damping, the logarithmic decrement over n cycles; plus a synthetic frame table (`"synthetic": true`) generated with a seeded release phase from which the learner's reduction must return the mean period, Type A and Type B uncertainties and E_n. **Oracle:** 4√(L/g) K(m) with m = sin²(θ₀/2) from `scipy.special.ellipk`; `solve_ivp` with events for the damped case. **Limit:** θ₀ → 10⁻³ rad must give 2π√(L/g) to 10⁻⁶. **Tolerance:** period rtol 10⁻⁷ undamped, so passing the modulus k instead of the parameter m (10% at 60°) and a degree/radian slip fail; the series must match the oracle to 10⁻³ below 60° and the case at 170° requires the learner to report that the series is *not* within 10⁻³ there (it is 25% low), so an implementation that uses the series everywhere fails; the reduced uncertainties to rtol 10⁻⁶ against the reference reduction, so dropping the √2 for two frame readings or the 1/√12 of a rectangular distribution fails.
- `p04-08-nodal-dc-*.json` (P04.08): a seeded resistor network of 3–30 nodes (random tree plus random extra edges, so loops are guaranteed) with 1–4 ideal voltage sources and current sources, and resistances log-uniform from 1 Ω to 1 MΩ → node voltages, branch currents, the power of every element, and the Thévenin equivalent between two named nodes. **Oracle:** an independent modified-nodal-analysis assembly solved by `numpy.linalg.solve`, plus the power balance. **Limit:** a single source and a single resistor must give Ohm's law exactly; a network of series resistors must give the voltage-divider ratios to 10⁻¹³. **Tolerance:** voltages rtol 10⁻⁹ with an absolute floor of 10⁻¹² V (the conductances span six decades, so the reduced conductance matrix has a condition number of order 10⁵–10⁶ in seeded trials and double precision leaves about ten figures; a tighter tolerance would sit too close to that floor), KCL at every node and the total power balance to 10⁻⁹ of the total power; a stamp with the wrong sign on an off-diagonal conductance, a missing ground row, or a voltage source stamped as a resistor fails the balance by orders of magnitude.
- `p04-10-rc-filter-*.json` (P04.10): R and C from E12 series values (seeded), a sampling rate, a list of test frequencies and a step input → magnitude in dB and phase at each frequency, the −3 dB frequency, the step response at given times, the alias frequency of each test tone, and for two buffered and two un-buffered stages the magnitude at one frequency. **Oracle:** the closed forms 1/(1 + jωRC), its step response 1 − e^{−t/RC} and the un-buffered two-stage transfer function 1/(1 + 3jωRC − ω²R²C²) (equal R and C) or its general form; `scipy.signal.freqs` as a second check. **Limit:** ω → 0 gives 0 dB and 0° exactly; ω = 1/RC gives −3.0103 dB and −45° to 10⁻⁹. **Tolerance:** magnitude atol 10⁻⁶ dB, phase atol 10⁻⁶ degrees; f_c to rtol 10⁻¹²; so 1/(RC) used as a frequency in hertz (a factor 2π), 10 log₁₀ in place of 20 log₁₀ for a voltage ratio, and the un-buffered cascade computed as the square of one stage (3.5 dB wrong at the corner, 2.6 dB at half and twice the corner frequency) all fail; the alias frequency must be folded into [0, f_s/2] exactly.
- `p04-09-biot-savart-loop-*.json` (P04.09): coil radius 10–200 mm, turns, current, and a seeded set of 20 field points on and off the axis, near and far → the field vector at each point, the dipole moment, and the torque in a given uniform field. **Oracle:** on the axis, μ₀NIa²/(2(a² + z²)^{3/2}); off the axis, the closed form in complete elliptic integrals (`scipy.special.ellipk`, `ellipe`); in the far field, the dipole formula. **Limit:** on the axis the transverse components must be zero to 10⁻¹² of |B| and the centre field μ₀NI/(2a) exact to 10⁻¹⁰; beyond 50a the result must match the dipole field to 10⁻³. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁴ on |B| at every point and atol 10⁻⁴|B| on each component, which a discretised Biot–Savart sum over 1000 midpoint segments meets at every point at least 0.1a from the wire (its error falls as the inverse square of the segment count: 1.9 × 10⁻⁵ at 1000 segments and 4.6 × 10⁻⁶ at 2000 for a point 0.2a outside the wire, 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ at 1000 for a point only 0.05a away) and which a sum with dl and r crossed in the wrong order (sign flip) or r³ replaced by r² fails; points within 0.1a of the wire are excluded, because the discretisation error of any finite sum grows without bound as the field point approaches a segment.
- `p04-11-dipole-friis-*.json` (P04.11): an antenna type (Hertzian, short, half-wave dipole), frequency, transmit power, gains, range and polarisation angle from the seed → the normalised pattern on a seeded grid of θ, the directivity, the half-power beamwidth, the free-space path loss, the received power in dBm, and the polarisation loss. **Oracle:** the pattern closed forms (sin²θ; cos²((π/2)cos θ)/sin²θ for the half-wave dipole) with directivity by `scipy.integrate.quad` of the pattern over the sphere (1.5 and 1.6409); the Friis equation in linear units. **Limit:** the isotropic case gives P_r = P_t(λ/(4πd))² exactly; the half-wave pattern's nulls at θ = 0 and π must be returned as 0, not NaN. **Tolerance:** directivity rtol 10⁻⁶ (a numerical integration with the sin θ Jacobian omitted gives 1.27 against 1.5, and with θ in degrees fails grossly), path loss atol 10⁻⁶ dB (so 4πd/λ not squared, or d in km, fails), beamwidth atol 0.01° from a root-find on the pattern (90.00° and 78.08° for the Hertzian and half-wave dipoles).
- `p04-13-gps-offset-*.json` (P04.13): an orbit radius 20 000–42 000 km and eccentricity 0–0.02 from the seed, ground latitude and height → the special-relativistic and gravitational fractional rates, the net offset per day, the factory frequency setting for a given nominal frequency, the periodic eccentricity correction at a given eccentric anomaly, and the 1-day ranging error. **Oracle:** the exact expressions (1 − 1/γ and the Schwarzschild ratio √(1 − 2GM/(c²r))/√(1 − 2GM/(c²R)), evaluated in extended precision with `mpmath`-free compensated arithmetic, i.e. `numpy.longdouble` and `log1p`/`expm1`), and the first-order formulas the lesson derives. **Limit:** for r = R and v = 0 every offset must be exactly zero; for GPS's 26 560 km circular orbit the net offset must be 38.5 μs/day to 0.1 μs. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻⁸ on each fractional rate, so the first-order expansion (whose relative error is about (3/4)v²/c² ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ here) passes while naive evaluation of 1 − √(1 − v²/c²) in double precision, which cancels about nine of the sixteen digits and is wrong by 4 × 10⁻⁷ relative at GPS speed (the Phase 03 lesson-01 trap), fails, as do a sign error on either term and GM/(c²r) with r taken as altitude instead of radius.
- `p04-14-halflife-dose-*.json` (P04.14): a nuclide from a seeded table (half-life, molar mass, decay energy), a mass, elapsed times from 0 to 10 half-lives, a two-member decay chain, a shield's mass attenuation coefficient and density with a thickness, and a source-to-target distance → the decay constant, activity, remaining fraction, decay power, daughter activity in the chain, transmitted fraction, half- and tenth-value thicknesses, and the dose rate scaled by inverse square from a reference point; plus a seeded Monte Carlo decay of N₀ = 10⁴ nuclei over a stated number of steps. **Oracle:** the exponential law and the Bateman solution for the chain in exact arithmetic (`fractions.Fraction` for the rational parts, `numpy.exp` at the end); the attenuation law I = I₀e^{−(μ/ρ)ρx}. **Limit:** t = 0 returns N₀ and the initial activity exactly; equal half-lives in the chain must be handled by the limiting form (te^{−λt}) and not divide by zero. **Tolerance:** rtol 10⁻¹⁰ on the deterministic outputs, so ln 2 forgotten in λ = ln 2/T_½ (a factor 0.69), years mixed with seconds, and μ/ρ used as μ (a factor ρ = 2.70 for aluminium) fail; the Monte Carlo survivor count must lie within 4 standard deviations of N₀e^{−λt} with σ = √(N₀p(1 − p)), a binomial bound (normal approximation, adequate at N₀ = 10⁴) that a correct implementation violates with probability 6 × 10⁻⁵ per checkpoint (one false failure in about 1600 cases across the 10 checkpoints), while a per-step decay probability λΔt used in place of 1 − e^{−λΔt} at λΔt = 0.5 fails it by more than 20σ.
