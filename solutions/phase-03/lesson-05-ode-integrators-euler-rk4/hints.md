# P03.05  Hints

One hint per problem. A hint points at the idea, not the answer. Read `method.md` next if the hint is not enough.

1. Each kᵢ is f evaluated at a predicted point; write the point first, then the slope. For (b) use `global_error(f, exact, (0, 2), h, method)` with `exact` returning a one-element array. The ratios approach 2ᵖ only as h → 0; the next term of the error expansion is what moves them.
2. (a) The error after one step from the exact solution is the Taylor remainder; the error recursion eₙ₊₁ ≤ (1 + hL)eₙ + h²M/2 sums as a geometric series, and (1 + hL)ⁿ ≤ e^{nhL}. (b) Substitute f = λy into the k's; the k's become λy times polynomials in z. (c) With f independent of y, k₂ = k₃. (d) Count steps: n = T/h of them, each adding O(h^{p+1}).
3. The right-hand side needs `abs` for the sign of drag; the state is [z, v]. Apogee: find the first index where v < 0, interpolate the time where v = 0 between the bracketing samples, then z there from z, v and the acceleration in the bracket. Euler's apogee error converges slowly: look at the ratios rather than a single fitted order.
4. `kepler_state(0, a, e)` is perigee; `kepler_state(T/2, a, e)` is apogee. `orbit_position_error(a, e, n_orbits, steps_per_orbit, method)` does everything in (b)–(d); `step_for_tolerance` uses the one-orbit error and order 4; for the day, multiply the one-orbit error by the number of orbits before extrapolating. `energy_drift(propagate_orbit(...)[1])[-1]` for (e).
5. `max_stable_step(order, -lam)`; the methods of order 1 and 2 share the limit 2. The exact solution lets you measure RK4's error at the largest stable step; its accuracy-only step comes from (2) with two runs. For (d) the oscillator has eigenvalues ±i, so z = ±ih and |1 + ih|² = 1 + h².
6. Scale the measured one-orbit errors: linearly with the number of orbits, then with (2) at the method's order. Evaluations = stages × steps × orbits × objects; divide by the machine rate. For e = 0.7 think about the perigee-to-apogee speed ratio; for (d) think about what rtol = 10⁻³ means in metres on a 7500 km orbit.
7. (a) Differentiate y′ = f(y) by the chain rule: y″ = f′f, y‴ = f″f² + f′²f. (b) k₂ = f(y + c₂hf) = f + c₂hf′f + c₂²h²f″f²/2 + …. (c) Matching h³ needs both f″f² and f′²f terms; the method produces only one of them. (d) Each stage is a polynomial in z times y; the step is therefore a polynomial of degree ≤ s; an order-p method agrees with e^z through zᵖ.
