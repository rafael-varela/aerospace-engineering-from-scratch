# P08.11  Hints

One hint per problem. A hint points at the idea, not the answer. Read `method.md` next if the hint is not enough.

1. At a stagnation point Ue = 0 and the quadrature is 0/0: take the limit with Ue = ax, which gives θ² = 0.075ν/a. Hiemenz is the Falkner–Skan solution with β = 1, whose similarity variable is η = y√(a/ν).
2. (a) Integrate from the wall to above the layer and replace v at the top by −∫u_x dy; add and subtract Ue to form the deficit integrands. (b) d(θ²)/dx = 2θ dθ/dx. (c) Integrate x^(5m) or (1 − x/L)⁵ in closed form. (d) Write x = Rφ and look at which combination of U, R and ν survives in λ.
3. (a) ∫(1 + ξ/L)^(−5)dξ is elementary. (b) θ₀² = 0.45νl₀/U₀ from the flat plate; λ₀ = θ₀²Ue′(0)/ν. (c) A piecewise Ue with Ue′ = 0 on the straight part and the diffuser's gradient after it.
4. Keep the similarity variables straight: Falkner–Skan uses η = y√((m + 1)Ue/(2νx)); convert before you compare.
5. The march is first order in x; use the trend of the four values. A separation point is where the wall shear goes to zero as √(x_s − x).
6. λ just behind the peak is θ_peak² × (dUe/dx)/ν: a thicker layer meeting a steeper gradient. After laminar separation the free shear layer is very unstable.
