# P08.01  Hints

One hint per problem. A hint points at the idea, not the answer. Read `method.md` next if the hint is not enough.

1. In a U-tube, follow the pressure from one surface down through the liquid and up the other leg; the air columns above each surface weigh something too, which is where ρ_f enters. In the accelerating tank, work in the tank's frame: the liquid feels g plus the acceleration.
2. (b) Write dp/p = −g₀ dh/(RT) and change variable from h to T with dT = a dh. (c) The second derivative of p at sea level involves both dρ/dh and the lapse rate; you only need the first derivative to identify the linear term. (d) Divide the pressure law by T.
3. The isothermal formula of Problem 2(b) applies with Mars's R and g; the scale height is RT/g. For (c), bracket the root between 0 and 47 km.
4. "Pressure altitude 3000 m" fixes the pressure, not the height. Density then follows from the ideal-gas law at the hot temperature. The true height is where the hot-day pressure profile reaches that pressure.
5. A zero-pressure balloon's net lift is (ρ_air − ρ_He) g V, and both densities scale with p/T in the same way when the gas is at ambient temperature.
6. For (c), a warmer layer has a larger scale height, so pressure falls more slowly with height there; the pressure-altitude error is the accumulated difference.
