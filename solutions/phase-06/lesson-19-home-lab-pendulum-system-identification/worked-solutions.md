# P06.19  Worked solutions

Each solution states: assumptions, units, the derivation, the result with its uncertainty and the method behind it, at least one independent check, and the common mistakes. Seeded problems are worked for s = 15; numerical results are from `solution.py` and probe scripts that call it, given to the figures shown. Every record used here is SYNTHETIC, generated by `make_synthetic_swing` or by `damped_cosine` plus seeded noise; none is a measurement.

## 1. The prediction (s = 15: L = 0.95 m)

**Assumptions.** Simple pendulum, point bob, sphere drag with C_d = 0.47, ρ = 1.2 kg/m³.

(a) **f₀ = 0.5114 ± 0.0008 Hz.** The length dominates: u_L/L = 0.32 %, u_g/g = 0.10 %; halved by the square root, 0.16 % and 0.05 %.

(b) f = **0.5105 Hz at 10°, 0.5075 Hz at 20°, 0.4959 Hz at 40°** (0.19 %, 0.76 %, 3.04 % below f₀). The differences, 0.57 % between 10° and 20° and 2.28 % between 20° and 40°, are about 80 and 300 times the 7 × 10⁻⁵ resolution of 60 crossings: **both are detectable**, comfortably. (Even 5° against 10°, 0.14 %, is about 20 times the resolution.)

(c) b₂ = ρC_d(πd²/4)L/(2m) = **0.00337 rad⁻¹**; **ζ_eq(20°) = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴**. With air drag alone, A(t) = A₀/(1 + βA₀t), β = 4b₂ω₀/(3π): after 120 s, **16.8°** from 20°.

**Check.** ζ_eq ∝ b₂A ∝ L/m at fixed A: longer strings and lighter bobs damp more.

**Common mistakes.** Using the bob's mass in the inertia but forgetting the extra L in the drag torque; quoting ζ for air drag without its amplitude.

## 2. Derivations

(a) With θ̇ = −Aω sin ωt over a period T = 2π/ω:
viscous ∮ b₁θ̇² dt = b₁A²ω² (π/ω) = πb₁A²ω; quadratic ∮ b₂|θ̇|³ dt = b₂A³ω³ (8/(3ω)) = (8/3)b₂A³ω²; Coulomb ∮ b₀|θ̇| dt = b₀Aω (4/ω) = 4b₀A.
With E = ½ω²A² per unit inertia, dE/dt = ω²A dA/dt = −(loss per cycle)/T, so
**dA/dt = −(b₁/2)A − (4b₂ω/(3π))A² − 2b₀/(πω)** and **ζ_eq = −(dA/dt)/(ωA) = b₁/(2ω) + (4b₂/(3π))A + 2b₀/(πω²A)**.

(b) A^(−p) dA = −κ dt gives A^(1−p)/(1 − p) = A₀^(1−p)/(1 − p) − κt, so **A = A₀[1 − (1 − p)κA₀^(p−1)t]^(1/(1−p))**; for p = 1, ln A = ln A₀ − κt, **A = A₀e^(−κt)**. p = 0: A = A₀ − κt, linear. p = 2: A^(−1) = A₀^(−1) + κt, so 1/A is linear.

(c) With u = 1/A, du/dt = −A⁻² dA/dt = αu + β. Then u + β/α = (1/A₀ + β/α)e^(αt), so **A = αA₀e^(−αt)/(α + βA₀(1 − e^(−αt)))**.

(d) δ = (ln A₀ − ln Aₙ)/n; each logarithm has standard uncertainty s (relative uncertainty of the peak), independent, so **u_δ = √2 s/n**. For small δ, ζ = δ/√(4π² + δ²) = (δ/2π)(1 + δ²/4π²)^(−1/2) ≈ δ/(2π).

**Common mistakes.** Averaging |sin|³ as ½ (it is 4/(3π)); forgetting that Coulomb loss per cycle is independent of frequency, so its dA/dt goes as 1/ω.

## 3. A wing mode in a ground vibration test (s = 15)

**Assumptions.** One mode, viscous damping, independent noise; SYNTHETIC record of 10 s at 256 Hz.

(a) NLS: **f_d = 4.1998 ± 0.0002 Hz**, **ζ = 0.01498 ± 0.00006** (covariance); residual RMS **0.0198 g** against the 0.02 g noise. The truth (4.2 Hz, 0.015) is within 1σ for f_d and 0.4σ for ζ.

(b) ln(peak) line over 42 peaks: **ζ = 0.0141 ± 0.0006** (1.3σ low); two-peak decrement over 20 cycles: **ζ = 0.0145**. Both are low because the late peaks, at 0.02–0.05 g, are comparable to the noise and the peak picker takes the largest sample in each lobe, which noise biases upward; the decay then looks slower. NLS uses every sample with the right weight and is not biased this way.

(c) Bootstrap sd of ζ **5.8 × 10⁻⁵**, against the covariance's 5.9 × 10⁻⁵: the covariance is **trustworthy**, because the residual RMS equals the noise, so the residuals are noise. The pendulum differs: its frequency and damping change with amplitude, the constant-parameter model is wrong at 20°, and its residuals (0.80° against 0.30°) are model error.

(d) **42 cycles**; the envelope has decayed to **0.015 g**, below the noise. A longer record adds only noise-dominated cycles and would not improve ζ; a second tap, or a higher-amplitude excitation, would.

**Common mistakes.** Trusting the log decrement in the noise-dominated tail; treating the covariance as wrong in every case after seeing it fail once.

## 4. How long a record? (committed SYNTHETIC record)

(a)

| record | ζ | u (cov.) | u (block) | ωₙ [rad/s] | u (cov.) | u (block) | RMS [°] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 1.0044 × 10⁻³ | 2.6 × 10⁻⁵ | 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ | 3.47779 | 9.2 × 10⁻⁵ | 1.3 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.317 |
| 60 s | 9.615 × 10⁻⁴ | 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ | 4.4 × 10⁻⁵ | 3.47990 | 4.2 × 10⁻⁵ | 1.3 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.393 |
| 120 s | 9.208 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.96 × 10⁻⁵ | 4.8 × 10⁻⁵ | 3.48333 | 3.3 × 10⁻⁵ | 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.800 |

(b) The covariance assumes independent residuals and falls roughly as 1/√N (and faster for ζ, as the lever arm in time grows). But the residual RMS grows from 0.32° (almost the 0.30° noise) to 0.80°: the longer the record, the more the true frequency and ζ drift away from the single values the model allows, and the residuals become a smooth, correlated model error. The block bootstrap sees that correlation, so its uncertainty does not fall. The estimates themselves also drift with record length (ζ from 1.00 × 10⁻³ to 0.92 × 10⁻³, ωₙ from 3.4778 to 3.4833 rad/s), because each is an average over a different amplitude range: they are not estimates of one constant.

(c) For a constant-parameter fit, about 30 s at 20° (where the model is adequate: RMS ≈ noise). Better, keep the long record and change the model: fit in windows (Problem 6), divide the frequency by the mean amplitude ratio, or fit the full nonlinear ODE with b₁, b₂ and L as parameters (Extension 1). A long record is what separates the damping mechanisms; it must not be thrown away to suit the model.

**Common mistakes.** Reporting the 120 s covariance as the uncertainty; comparing ωₙ from records of different length as if they measured the same quantity.

## 5. Tell the damping apart (s = 15)

(a) **p = 0.10 ± 0.04** (Coulomb; amplitude 29.6° → 17.6°), **1.08 ± 0.03** (viscous; 29.4° → 16.8°), **2.00 ± 0.02** (quadratic; 28.8° → 12.6°). Each is within a few standard errors of 0, 1 and 2, and the three are separated by more than 25σ. (The Coulomb and viscous values sit 2–3σ from their nominal exponents, which reflects the cycle-averaging approximation and the peak picker, not a different mechanism.)

(b) 40° for 150 s: `fit_envelope_vq` gives **b₁ = 0.00958 ± 0.00023 s⁻¹** (truth 0.01, 1.8σ) and **b₂ = 0.02038 ± 0.00025 rad⁻¹** (truth 0.02, 1.5σ), correlation −0.97. `fit_envelope` gives **p = 1.649 ± 0.009**: a precise number with no physical meaning. A mixture of viscous and quadratic damping has no single exponent; p is the compromise that best matches the curvature over this amplitude range, and it would change if the range changed. Report b₁ and b₂ for a mixture; use p only to rule mechanisms in or out.

(c) 20° for 120 s (decaying to 5.9°): b₁ = 0.0096 ± 0.0006 s⁻¹, b₂ = 0.0200 ± 0.0010 rad⁻¹, correlation −0.99. Halving the release amplitude (and shortening the record) multiplies the uncertainties by about three to four and pushes the correlation towards −1: over a narrower range of A, the viscous (constant) and quadratic (∝ A) parts of ζ_eq(A) look more alike. The lesson's committed record, 20° to 13.5°, is the extreme case: correlation −1.00.

**Common mistakes.** Reading p = 1.65 as "the drag law is A^1.65"; fitting the mixed model to a narrow amplitude range and trusting the individual coefficients.

## 6. Which uncertainty do you report?

(a) **The block bootstrap, 0.48 × 10⁻⁴**, with the residual RMS stated beside it. The residual RMS is 2.7 times the known noise, so most of the residual is model error, correlated over a swing. The covariance and the single-residual bootstrap both treat the residuals as independent and so understate the uncertainty about fivefold. Repeat runs with fresh noise have the *same* model error in every run, so their scatter (0.05 × 10⁻⁴) measures only the noise. A bias common to all repeats never shows in their scatter.

(b) Windows of 30 s on the committed SYNTHETIC record (covariance uncertainties):

| window | mean amplitude | ζ | truth ζ_eq (generator) | RMS [°] | f₀ [Hz] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–30 s | 19.08° | (1.004 ± 0.026) × 10⁻³ | 0.994 × 10⁻³ | 0.317 | 0.55737 ± 0.00001 |
| 30–60 s | 16.98° | (0.994 ± 0.028) × 10⁻³ | 0.931 × 10⁻³ | 0.307 | 0.55726 ± 0.00002 |
| 60–90 s | 15.97° | (0.883 ± 0.030) × 10⁻³ | 0.902 × 10⁻³ | 0.296 | 0.55746 ± 0.00002 |
| 90–120 s | 14.14° | (0.899 ± 0.033) × 10⁻³ | 0.847 × 10⁻³ | 0.303 | 0.55734 ± 0.00002 |

The RMS equals the 0.30° noise in every window, so the damped cosine is **adequate within a window**. ζ falls with amplitude as the generator's ζ_eq(A) = b₁/(2ω) + (4b₂/3π)A does, within 0.4σ, 2.2σ, 0.6σ and 1.6σ of the truth: roughly consistent, with one window at 2.2σ.

(c) The weighted mean is **0.557355 ± 0.000008 Hz**, but χ² = **81** for 3 degrees of freedom: the four values scatter far more than their covariance uncertainties allow. Even in short windows the frequency estimate carries small model errors (the amplitude still changes by 1–2° within a window, and the amplitude correction uses mean peaks). The honest uncertainty is the scatter: standard deviation 8.3 × 10⁻⁵ Hz, standard error **4.2 × 10⁻⁵ Hz**. Report **f₀ = 0.55736 ± 0.00004 Hz** (scatter of four windows), consistent with the generator's 0.55733 Hz and with the whole-record estimate 0.55732 Hz.

**Common mistakes.** Choosing the smallest uncertainty because it is the most "precise"; combining windows with a weighted mean without checking χ².

## Check-yourself answers

1. δ = (1/n) ln(A₀/Aₙ) from two peaks n cycles apart; ζ = δ/√(4π² + δ²) ≈ δ/(2π).
2. A pendulum's restoring torque grows as sin θ, slower than θ, so larger swings take longer; as the amplitude decays the period shortens and the frequency rises. At 20° the frequency is 0.76 % below the small-angle value.
3. Viscous: ζ_eq independent of amplitude. Quadratic: ζ_eq ∝ A. Coulomb: ζ_eq ∝ 1/A.
4. Coulomb (dry) friction: a constant amplitude loss per cycle. Look at the pivot: a string rubbing over a rod, a loose clip, a hinge.
5. The residuals are mostly model error, not noise, so the covariance (which assumes independent residuals) understates the uncertainty. Use a block bootstrap, fit in windows where the model is adequate, or improve the model, and report the residual RMS with the result.
6. Each repeat contains the same model error (and the same systematic errors of the setup), so the repeats agree with each other while all being wrong in the same way; their scatter measures only what changes between runs.
7. The input of the prediction: here, the effective length L = g/(2πf₀)² (or g, if L is known better), since the measurement is now the more precise side of the comparison.
8. So that the comparison tests the model honestly: a prediction written after the data cannot be falsified by them, and an unrecorded prediction tends to drift towards the measured value.
