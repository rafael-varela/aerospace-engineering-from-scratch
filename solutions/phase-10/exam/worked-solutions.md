# Phase 10 Exam: worked solutions and pass thresholds

Attempt the exam first, in the time you set yourself. Then grade each question against this key and record the result per competency (1 reasoning, 2 analysis, 3 computation, 4 interpretation). **Pass threshold:** proficient (3 of 4) or better on every competency. "Proficient" means the reasoning is right and the result is right to within rounding, even if the presentation is rough. In Part B a correct final number reached by a wrong derivation is *not* proficient: the derivation, the statement of where the model sits in the hierarchy and what it neglects, and the independent check are what the question tests. In Part C the tests must actually pass. In Part D a correct normalised error with the wrong diagnosis drawn from it is not proficient either. An answer that presents a synthetic table as if it were a measurement, or a computation as if it were data, fails the question regardless of its arithmetic.

## Part A

**1.** (a) q = ½ρV² (Pa), the kinetic energy per unit volume of the free stream and the pressure rise at a stagnation point. cl = L′/(qc), cd = D′/(qc), cm = M′/(qc²) per unit span for a section; CL = L/(qS), CD = D/(qS), Cm = M/(qSc̄) for a wing or aircraft, with S and c̄ stated. Re = ρVl/μ = Vl/ν (inertial over viscous forces at the scale l); M = V/a (flow speed over the speed of sound). The force on a body of fixed shape and attitude depends on ρ, V, l, μ and a: six quantities including F, three units, so by the Buckingham Π theorem (`P08.05`) there are three independent groups, F/(½ρV²l²) = f(Re, M). Two flows with the same Re and M have the same coefficients. The trainer cruises at Re = 4.52 × 10⁶ on its 1.2 m chord, M = 0.162. A 0.25 m chord model would need 264 m/s for the same Re, which is M = 0.78: compressibility would change the flow completely. In an atmospheric tunnel Re and M cannot both be matched, and low-speed testing matches M approximately (both are incompressible) and accepts a lower Re.
(b) 2-D sections: thin-airfoil theory (neglects thickness, viscosity, large angles; checked against the panel method as t → 0, where the panel cl tends to it from above); the vortex panel method (inviscid, attached flow, no drag; checked against the exact Joukowski/Karman–Trefftz solution with an observed order near 2); viscous–inviscid coupling with Thwaites, Michel, Head and Squire–Young (one-way, attached flow, empirical transition; each closure checked against Falkner–Skan or the power-law plate, the chain against Ladson's data). 3-D wings: the horseshoe and lifting line (high aspect ratio, unswept, flat wake, linear sections; checked against the elliptic closed form CL = 2πα AR/(AR + 2), e = 1, with the collocation order measured); the vortex lattice (thin flat surface, flat wake; checked against lifting line for unswept high-AR wings and by its own lattice convergence). Rotors: actuator-disk momentum theory (ideal, uniform inflow, no blades); blade element and BEM (annuli independent, 2-D sections, tip loss as a correction; checked against momentum theory in the ideal limit, where a uniform-inflow blade must reproduce it exactly). Time: quasi-steady theory; Theodorsen's frequency response and Wagner's step response (flat plate, attached flow, flat wake; Theodorsen checked against its quasi-steady limit k → 0, and Wagner against Theodorsen through C(k) = ikΦ(ik)).
(c) Code verification shows that the equations are solved correctly: a symmetric section gives cl = 0 at α = 0 to round-off, or the panel method converges to the exact Karman–Trefftz cl at order 2 (lesson 5). Solution verification estimates the numerical error of one particular answer: the GCI of a lattice CL_α from three lattices (lesson 22). Validation compares the model's answer with a measurement, with every uncertainty on the table, for a stated purpose and range of conditions: lesson 22's lattice against twelve NACA planforms. "Agrees" is not a statement: it needs the comparison error, the numerical, input and data uncertainties, the metric, and the domain in which it holds. A code can agree by compensating errors, or because the uncertainty band is so wide that nothing could fail it.
(d) The lift slope, the zero-lift angle and the aerodynamic centre are set mainly by the potential flow: they change by a few per cent with Re through the boundary layer's displacement effect, and transfer with small corrections. The minimum drag and cl_max are set by the boundary layer (transition, separation) and do not transfer. Between the bench (Re ≈ 2.7 × 10⁴) and the trainer (4.52 × 10⁶), a factor of 170, the bench section separates laminar: its slope is lower, its cd several times higher, its stall earlier and softer. Even the "transferable" quantities shift at that gap. *Proficient:* the definitions with the Π-theorem argument, Re 4.5 × 10⁶ and M 0.16, the 264 m/s / M 0.78 impossibility; all four families with what each neglects and its independent check; the three V&V terms with examples and why "agrees" is empty; the potential-flow/boundary-layer split and its consequence at a factor 170. *Common mistakes:* listing models without their checks; saying a matched Re is enough when M is not; calling a grid-convergence study "validation".

**2.** (a) Γ = ∮V·dl, taken positive clockwise for a lifting airfoil in a left-to-right stream, so that L′ = ρV∞Γ > 0. Kutta–Joukowski: the lift per unit span of any 2-D body in steady potential flow is L′ = ρV∞Γ, perpendicular to the stream, with zero drag. Laplace's equation with the surface as a streamline has a solution for every Γ: adding a point vortex inside the body keeps the boundary condition. Potential theory cannot choose. The Kutta condition requires the flow to leave a sharp trailing edge smoothly, with finite velocity there. This fixes Γ: Γ = 4πVa sin(α + β) for a Joukowski section. Physically, an airfoil starting from rest has zero circulation round a large loop. As it starts, the boundary layer at the trailing edge sheds a starting vortex until the flow leaves the edge smoothly. Kelvin's theorem keeps the loop's total at zero, so an equal and opposite circulation stays bound to the airfoil. (b) Helmholtz: the strength of a vortex filament is constant along it, and a filament cannot end in the fluid (it closes, goes to infinity or ends on a boundary). A bound vortex of finite span must therefore turn downstream at its ends as two trailing legs of the same Γ: the horseshoe. A continuous span load sheds a trailing sheet of strength −dΓ/dy. The trailing vortices induce a downwash at the wing, which tilts each section's lift backwards by α_i = w/V. The rearward component is induced drag, the rate at which kinetic energy is left in the wake. A 2-D airfoil leaves no wake vorticity in steady inviscid flow, so its drag is zero (d'Alembert). (c) Far downstream each trailing leg is a complete infinite line, while at the lifting line it is semi-infinite and starts at the point's own plane, giving exactly half (the factor 1 + cos 90° = 1 in the semi-infinite law). So w_T = 2w for a flat wake. For a given lift and span, induced drag ∫Γw dy is least when w is uniform along the span. Glauert's integral shows this happens exactly for Γ ∝ √(1 − (2y/b)²): the elliptic load, e = 1, and any other load has e = 1/(1 + δ) < 1. A planar wing with e = 1.03 is a numerical error (lesson 9's y-midpoint control points produce exactly this), not a discovery. At fixed weight and speed, D_i = L²/(½ρV²πeb²): the span, not the area or the aspect ratio by itself. (d) Thin-airfoil theory replaces the section by a vortex sheet on the chord, and α enters only through the A₀ term, whose lift is 2πα with its centre at c/4. Camber adds the A₁, A₂ terms, which shift the lift line and add a constant c_m,c/4 = (π/4)(A₂ − A₁), so the quarter chord is the aerodynamic centre. Inviscid thickness raises the slope: the panel method gives 1.099 × 2π for the 12 % NACA 2412 and 1.0987 × 2π for the 0012 (0.12049 per degree), about +10 %. The boundary layer, thicker on the suction side, decambers the section: Ladson's 0012 at Re = 2 × 10⁶ has 0.10355 per degree = 0.944 × 2π over −4.5° to 8.5°, about 6 % below 2π and 14 % below the inviscid thick-section value. *Proficient:* the sign convention, the one-parameter family and the starting-vortex/Kelvin argument; Helmholtz to the horseshoe, induced drag as the lift tilt and wake energy; the factor 2, uniform downwash for the elliptic load, e > 1 as an alarm, span controls D_i; 2π and c/4 from the Fourier solution, thickness up about 10 %, viscosity down to about 0.94 × 2π. *Common mistakes:* calling Γ positive counter-clockwise and getting negative lift; "the Kutta condition is a law of physics"; saying aspect ratio rather than span controls induced drag at fixed weight and speed.

**3.** (a) δ* = ∫(1 − u/u_e) dy, the distance the wall is displaced outward; θ = ∫(u/u_e)(1 − u/u_e) dy, the momentum deficit; H = δ*/θ (about 2.6 laminar on a flat plate, 1.3–1.4 turbulent, rising towards separation). Momentum integral: dθ/ds + (2 + H)(θ/u_e) du_e/ds = c_f/2. Thwaites gives the laminar θ in closed form, θ²u_e⁶ = 0.45ν∫u_e⁵ds, with H(λ) and c_f(λ) from fits and laminar separation at λ = −0.09. Michel's correlation Re_θ ≥ 1.174(1 + 22 400/Re_x)Re_x^0.46 places transition. Head's entrainment equation with Ludwieg–Tillmann friction closes the turbulent layer. Squire–Young converts the trailing-edge θ, H and u_e into the far-wake momentum deficit, cd = 2(θ_TE/c)(u_e,TE/V)^{(H+5)/2} per surface. One-way coupling computes the boundary layer on the inviscid velocity but never feeds δ* back. The decambering effect of the thicker suction-side layer, which lowers the circulation, is therefore missing, and the predicted slope stays at its inviscid value (lesson 21: +12.7 % at 5.97 × 10⁶, +16.4 % at 2 × 10⁶). (b) Laminar separation: an adverse pressure gradient stops the slow fluid near the wall of a laminar layer, which has little momentum there. Transition: the growth of disturbances turns the layer turbulent, and turbulent mixing brings momentum to the wall, so a turbulent layer tolerates much more adverse gradient. A laminar separation bubble forms when the laminar layer separates, the free shear layer turns turbulent within a distance ℓ, and it reattaches. In Thwaites' integral θ² ∝ ν at every station, so λ = θ²u_e′/ν does not depend on ν: the separation point depends only on the shape of u_e(s), that is on the section and α. What depends on Re is whether transition comes first, since Re_θ grows as √Re and Re_x as Re. A bubble closes if separation plus ℓ lies ahead of the trailing edge; ℓ = N_ℓν/u_e,s shrinks as 1/Re. (c) TN-2502: trailing-edge stall (separation creeping forward from the trailing edge; thick sections, gentle); leading-edge stall (a short bubble near the nose bursts; moderately thin sections, abrupt); thin-airfoil stall (a long bubble growing from a sharp nose; very thin sections, gradual). The NACA 0012 at 2 × 10⁶ stalls from the leading edge: Ladson's cl falls from 1.408 at 15.25° to 0.753 at 16.25°. The Kirchhoff model describes trailing-edge separation through f(α), so fitted to an abrupt drop its sigmoid width collapses (S = 0.281°). On an untwisted rectangular wing the tip vortices unload the outer sections, so the local cl is highest at the root (1.12 CL), which stalls first. Taper loads the outer wing and moves the critical station outboard (η = 0.53 at taper 0.5). Washout lowers the tips' angle and moves it back inboard. (d) The turbulent estimate assumes turbulent flow from the leading edge, so it falls as C_f(Re) does: from 0.00987 at 2 × 10⁶ to 0.00681 at 1.89 × 10⁷ (−31.0 %). In the low-turbulence tunnel the measured section keeps a laminar run whose length shrinks as Re rises: the matching transition moves from 46 % of the chord at 2 × 10⁶ to 3 % at 1.89 × 10⁷ (lesson 13). Lower friction from the higher Re and more turbulent surface from earlier transition nearly cancel. cl_max is set by separation physics. Below about 5 × 10⁵ the mechanism changes (laminar separation, bubbles, bursting), and Ladson's data stop at 2 × 10⁶, so a fit in log Re cannot be carried across the change of mechanism to a drone at 1.6 × 10⁵. *Proficient:* the three definitions and the momentum integral, each closure's role, and why one-way coupling misses the slope loss; the λ argument for Re independence and the bubble closure condition; the three stall types, the 0012 as leading-edge stall, the near-step fit, root stall with the taper and washout effects; the transition-cancellation explanation with the 31 % and the change of mechanism for cl_max. *Common mistakes:* "the separation point moves aft as Re grows" (in Thwaites, only transition does); explaining the constant measured cd by "experimental error".

**4.** (a) Mass: ṁ = ρA(V + v). Momentum: T = ṁw, with w the far-wake increase. Energy: P = ½ṁ((V + w)² − V²). Work at the disk: P = T(V + v). Equating the two expressions for P with T = ṁw gives V + v = V + w/2, so w = 2v. The power of an ideal disk is the least for its thrust: any real propeller adds swirl, profile drag, tip loss and non-uniform inflow, and at fixed momentum flux a non-uniform velocity increase carries more kinetic energy than a uniform one. So η ≤ η_i = V/(V + v). Static ideal power P = T^{3/2}/√(2ρA): doubling T multiplies it by 2^{3/2} = 2.83; doubling the diameter quadruples A and multiplies it by 1/2. FM = P_ideal/P. For a given rotor the profile power is roughly fixed while the induced power grows as T^{3/2}, so FM rises with loading, and FM compares rotors fairly only at equal disk loading. (b) In the cross-flow plane each section moves sideways at w = Vα and carries vertical momentum ρS(x)w (its apparent mass times w). A slab of air passing at speed V gains ρV(dS/dx)w per unit length, so the body feels dN/dx = 2qα dS/dx. Integrated, N = 2qα(S(L) − S(0)), zero for a closed body: the nose's upward loading and the tail's downward loading cancel. Their moment does not cancel. By parts, M = 2qα·Vol, a couple, the same about every point because a couple has no line of action. It is nose-up for positive α, so destabilising: it moves the neutral point forward (lesson 16: 0.505c to between 0.457c and 0.412c for the trainer), and the tail or the centre-of-gravity range must pay for it. (c) k = ωb/V, the phase the motion advances while the air travels one semi-chord; s = Vt/b, the distance travelled in semi-chords. C(0) = 1 (quasi-steady: the wake has had time to settle) and C(∞) = ½ (the wake removes half the circulatory lift). Wagner: φ(0) = ½ (half the final circulatory lift appears at once) and φ(∞) = 1. They describe one linear system: C(k) = ikΦ(ik), with Φ the Laplace transform of φ. Theodorsen's function is the frequency response of Wagner's step response. The lift of a pitching section can lead the pitch at moderate k because its α̇ terms (the three-quarter-chord downwash and the apparent mass) lead α by 90°. At k = 0.343 they outweigh the wake's lag, so the trainer's section leads by 18°. (d) The ground is a plane with zero normal velocity. A mirror-image wing with reversed circulation cancels the vertical velocity on the plane exactly. Its upwash at the real wing lowers the downwash: at fixed α the tangency condition needs more circulation (more lift), and at fixed lift the Trefftz-plane downwash, hence the induced drag, is lower. During a real flare the wake was shed higher up, where the ground's effect was weaker, so the steady result overstates the cushion. A flat lattice cannot represent the channel flow under a thick or flapped wing very close to the ground, which can reduce lift, or the descent itself. *Proficient:* the w = 2v derivation from all three conservation laws, the bound and its reason, 2.83 and 1/2, FM and its loading dependence; the apparent-mass derivation, zero lift and a reference-free destabilising couple, the neutral-point consequence; k, s, the four limits with meanings, C = ikΦ(ik), the α̇ reason for lead; the image argument for both effects, the upper-bound reason and one missing effect. *Common mistakes:* deriving w = 2v from momentum alone; "a closed body has no aerodynamic effect"; using the chord instead of the semi-chord in k.

```python
import numpy as np
import pytest


def lifting_line(chord, b, alpha, N, a0=2 * np.pi, alpha_L0=0.0):
    """Prandtl lifting line by Glauert collocation for a symmetric, untwisted straight wing.

    chord(y) -> local chord; b span; alpha (rad) geometric angle; N odd coefficients A_1, A_3, ...,
    A_(2N-1), collocated at theta_i = i pi / (2N), i = 1..N (half span, last point at the root).
    Monoplane equation: sum A_n sin(n theta)(mu n + sin theta) = mu (alpha - alpha_L0) sin theta,
    mu = a0 c / (4 b).  Returns dict(CL, CDi, e, A, n, AR)."""
    n = 2 * np.arange(N) + 1
    th = np.arange(1, N + 1) * np.pi / (2 * N)
    y = -0.5 * b * np.cos(th)
    mu = a0 * chord(y) / (4 * b)
    M = np.sin(np.outer(th, n)) * (mu[:, None] * n[None, :] + np.sin(th)[:, None])
    A = np.linalg.solve(M, mu * (alpha - alpha_L0) * np.sin(th))
    t = np.linspace(0.0, np.pi, 20001)
    S = np.trapezoid(chord(-0.5 * b * np.cos(t)) * 0.5 * b * np.sin(t), t)
    AR = b ** 2 / S
    CL = np.pi * AR * A[0]
    CDi = np.pi * AR * np.sum(n * A ** 2)
    return dict(CL=CL, CDi=CDi, e=CL ** 2 / (np.pi * AR * CDi), A=A, n=n, AR=AR)
```
